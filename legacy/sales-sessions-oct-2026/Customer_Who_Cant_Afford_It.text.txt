The core idea
“I can't afford it” almost never means they can't pay.
They don't see the value yet.
They're scared of the number.
Nobody gave them a way to say yes.
Our job is to remove the barrier, not drop the price.

Part 1
The ten-point masterclass
Ten habits for the customer who says money's tight. Each one you can use on your very next job.
Sales Masterclass
4
Point 1 of 10
Never prejudge
A modest house is not a modest budget. Decide what they can afford before they speak, and you've taken away their choice and our revenue.
Prejudging is the most expensive objection, because we raise it for the customer.
Say it like this
Same four options, same confidence, every door. The customer decides what they can afford. Not us.
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
5
Point 2 of 10
Separate can't from won't
Most of the time it's “I don't want to spend that”, not “I can't”. Stay calm and curious, never apologetic. Then let them talk.
Their answer tells you which tool to reach for next.
Say it like this
Totally fair. Can I ask what feels like too much about it?
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
6
Point 3 of 10
Always give the four options
One price is a yes or no. Four options turn the question into “which one?”
Elite
The best outcome. Present it first.
Premium
Where most homes land.
Standard
A solid, sensible choice.
Essential
Bare bones. Fixes today's problem.
Don't read it like a menu. Anchor at Elite, then narrow to the two that fit this home.
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
7
Point 4 of 10
Payment options are the real unlock
Most people don't have a lump sum sitting there. Almost everyone has a weekly number.
What scares them
$2,000
What they can say yes to
~$40 a week
Illustrative: $2,000 over 12 months before interest and fees. Quote our finance partner's real figures.
8
Point 5 of 10
Sell the cost of doing nothing
The leak doesn't pause while they save up.
Today
A planned fix at today's price.
In three months
Water damage, and a bigger bill.
The worst night
An emergency call-out at after-hours rates.
“Honestly, what happens if we leave it is the part I'd worry about.”
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
9
Point 6 of 10
Say the price. Then stop talking.
The silence feels long to you, not to them. Most techs discount before the customer has even objected.
Count to five in your head if you need to.
Say it like this
[State the price.] … [Wait. Let them speak first.]

10
Point 7 of 10
Feel, felt, found
An old line, and it still works. It lets you disagree with the customer without arguing with them.
Say it like this
I understand how you feel. Plenty of our customers felt the same. What they found was…
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
11
Point 8 of 10
Never discount for free
If the price comes down, something comes out of the job. Otherwise they learn our first number was fake, and they'll always push.
Say it like this
I can't do that price on the full job. If we do just the urgent part today, here's where we land.
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
12
Point 9 of 10
Anchor high: Elite first
Lead with the top option, with full confidence. Everything below it sounds reasonable. Lead with Essential, and everything above it sounds like a rip-off.
Say it like this
The option I'd put in my own home is Elite. Let me show you why, and then what sits below it.
Part 1 · Sales Masterclass
13
Point 10 of 10
Letting a family walk away with a dodgy patch, because we were too soft to have the money conversation, is not kindness.
Selling is service. If the fix protects their home, recommending it is the job.

Part 3 · Dispatch Pro
The rules it plays by
Be on a regular shift
Dispatch Pro only assigns techs on regular shifts. On-call shifts are ignored.
Have the skills on file
You need every skill the job type lists. Check yours with dispatch.
Drive time is a hard limit
It never sends a tech past the maximum drive time.
Every job counts
Recall and warranty jobs are left out. Every other job feeds your score.
Source: ServiceTitan Dispatch Pro documentation. Saturday shifts need to be rostered as regular shifts to count.
