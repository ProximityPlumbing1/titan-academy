- Every tech has their own login and works through their lessons on their phone at their own pace, any time.

### Certification
**Certificate:** Proximity Certified Install Technician / Proximity Certified Service Technician / Proximity Advanced Sales Certification

**Requires:**
- Install and Service: Field Foundations complete
- Service: Estimating & Selling Foundations complete
- Sales: x1–x8 complete (the refresh module can be tested out)
- Every module in their role path complete: lessons, quiz ≥80%, simulation passed, all field sign-offs signed
- Final assessment ≥80%: 25 scenario questions drawn from their whole program, taken on their phone in the week 8 session (one retake allowed). Sales: an advanced scenario assessment plus a passed live role-play round.

**The certificate:**
- Tech's name, role, completion date, a unique certificate number and William's signature
- QR code linking to a verification page (valid / not valid)
- Download as PDF and print-ready (A4 landscape); also shown on the tech's profile
- Apprentices: 'Supervised Competency, Install', reissued as full certification once licensed

**Not finished by week 8:** Anyone not complete by the end of week 8 gets a 2-week extension for outstanding sign-offs. Their certificate is issued automatically when the last item is signed.

### 1. What's Changed, Your Two Numbers & Dispatch Pro `x1`
**Goal:** Lock in the current rules, and see exactly how average ticket and close rate drive pay and the jobs you get.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 Masterclass Parts 2–3, selling/e1–e5 (condensed)
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 Sales Masterclass

**Lessons**
1. Kill complacency: where experienced sellers leave money on the table (autopilot, same pitch, guessing the budget)
2. The rules now: the promise (fixed price, lifetime labour warranty, same-day return), four options Elite first, every line from the pricebook, 50% deposit, no free discounts. Only Harriet or Sadie can change a price
3. Your two numbers: average ticket × close rate. Ten jobs at $2,000 vs eleven at $2,200 is +21%, because they multiply

**Key points to teach**

### 2. The Clean Handover: Sell It, Hand It Over, Get Back to Selling `x2`
**Goal:** Your job is to sell. Hand over every sold job so completely that the Install Manager and installers never need to call you.
*Reuse content from:* selling/e5 (handover)

**Lessons**
1. Who does what: you sell. The Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) owns delivery, the customer's happiness and the balance payment. (Service Techs captain the planned jobs they sell, but Sales Techs don't.)
2. Why the handover is the last part of the sale: an incomplete handover is the only thing that drags you off the road and away from your next lead
3. The handover pack: scope (in and out), materials and specials, photos and video, access, estimated hours, customer notes and expectations
4. Signed approval and the 50% deposit collected by you at signing, before you leave, then booked with Kim. No deposit, no booking and no materials ordered
5. Setting the customer up: who'll contact them next (the Install Manager), what happens on install day, and that the balance is due on completion
6. Scope accuracy: your hours set the installer's pay, and under-scoping causes the scope-change calls and warranty returns that land back on you

**Key points to teach**
- Sales Techs only sell. After a complete handover, the job belongs to the Install Manager.
- The test: could the installer do this job without ever calling you? If not, the handover isn't finished.
- Commission is paid on sales paid in full, so a clean handover protects your pay as well as the installer's.
- You collect the 50% deposit at signing. The Install Manager collects the balance on completion.

**Simulation: 🗂️ The rushed handover.** Last lead of a long day. The customer signed a $16K Elite bathroom job and you're tempted to submit the handover with 'see photos' as the scope.
Strong moves: Finish the scope (in and out) properly → Record access, specials and the customer's expectations → Tell the customer the Install Manager will call them next

### 3. One Problem, Never One Job: Bundling & Never Prejudging `x3`
**Goal:** Every quote solves the whole problem and every customer sees every option, whatever the house looks like.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 'Selling the Big Job' ch. 2 & 5, Oct 2026 Masterclass points 1 & 3, service/s2 (health check)
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 sessions; Joe Crisara / Service MVP: diagnose the person, not just the equipment; Nexstar: explore the customer's lifestyle and needs before presenting

**Lessons**
1. Never prejudge: a modest house is not a modest budget, and you can't see wealth or debt from the driveway
2. Present every option, every time, at every door. Their choice reveals the budget, not your guess
3. Frame the system: an ageing system failing in stages. Patching one part moves the leak to the next weak point
4. The bundling rule: never quote one job. Build the tiers as bundles from what you found (photos, video, CCTV)
5. The five standard bundles: Hot Water, Whole-Home Water Protection, Bathroom Refresh, Drain Fix-for-Good, Landlord Compliance Pack
6. Build options around what they told you matters, not what we'd like to sell. Whole job or small first: high trust, go whole; low trust, earn it with one great job, never go small out of fear of the number
7. Pricing: every line from the pricebook, no bundle discount. Cheaper means fewer lines, never a lower price
8. Turning repeat bundles into ServiceTitan templates

**Key points to teach**
- Prejudging is the most expensive objection, because we raise it for the customer.
- Every quote has at least one bundled tier, built from findings, never from a script.
- No bundle discounts, ever. Every line is priced from the pricebook.
- If you've quoted the same bundle twice, it should be a template (reviewed and signed off before going live).

**Simulation: 🔥 'Just replace the hot water'.** A tidy older home and a quiet, careful owner. The 14-year-old unit has failed; there's no tempering valve, perished flexis and high mains pressure. They say 'just the hot water, mate'.
Strong moves: Don't prejudge the budget → Show the findings as one ageing system, not a list → Present all four, Elite first (Essential: unit only; Elite: full bundle with a Care Plan), and let them choose

### 4. Trust, Discovery & the Cost of Doing Nothing `x4`
**Goal:** Earn the right to present: read the trust shift, ask better questions, and make the consequences real.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 'Selling the Big Job' ch. 3, Oct 2026 Masterclass point 5
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 sessions; Joe Crisara: listen more than you talk; people buy peace of mind; find the value gap; Sandler: the up-front contract (agree what happens on this visit, including that 'no' is OK); RAIN Group: uncover goals (aspirations) as well as problems (afflictions); NEPQ (Jeremy Miner): curious, question-led discovery and consequence questions

**Lessons**
1. The up-front agreement on arrival: what you'll do, how long, and that at the end you'll show options and they can say yes, no or not yet
2. Read the trust signals: forward questions ('how long will it last?', 'when can you start?'), open body language, and new problems they start telling you about
3. Present after the shift, not before. Presenting too early is how big jobs go cold
4. Discovery for big decisions: how long they'll stay, what's annoyed them, what they want the house to be like, who decides
5. Diagnose the person: how the problem affects the people in the house
6. Consequence questions and the cost of doing nothing: today's planned fix, water damage in three months, the 2am emergency at after-hours rates

**Key points to teach**
- Ask, then listen. Technicians who talk too much miss the details that justify the bigger option.
- Customers buy peace of mind, not parts. The value gap closes when they see what the fix means for them.
- Consequences, not scare tactics: describe what really happens if they leave it, calmly and honestly.
- The up-front agreement makes 'not yet' a normal outcome, which takes the pressure off both sides.

**Simulation: 🧐 The polite but guarded customer.** Says all the right things but asks only defensive questions. You're tempted to present now.
Strong moves: Hold off presenting → Ask about how the problem affects them and what they'd like → Present once they start asking forward questions

**Field sign-off** (signed off by: Harriet (Sales Manager); William as backup)
- [ ] Observed visit (ride-along or recorded) showing the up-front agreement and at least three discovery questions before any price

### 5. Presenting the Big Number `x5`
**Goal:** Make doing it properly once feel like the obvious choice, then hold the silence.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 'Selling the Big Job' ch. 7–8, Oct 2026 Masterclass points 6 & 9
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 sessions; Behavioural research cited in the sessions (anchoring, loss aversion, the decoy effect)

**Lessons**
1. Anchor honestly: walk through total failure first (the burst, the ceiling, the damage bill), then the full fix
2. Elite is the hero: the 'do it properly once' option. Present it first so the options below it are visibly compromises
3. Elite first, with full confidence ('the option I'd put in my own home'), then narrow to the two that fit this home. Don't read it like a menu
4. Let them choose up: people want to be the one who says 'I'd rather just do it properly once'
5. Scope. Price. Silence. Top sellers pause about two seconds after the price. The silence feels long to you, not to them
6. Never justify and never discount to fill the quiet. Whoever speaks next should be the customer

**Key points to teach**
- The first number they hear sets the scale. Lead with Elite and everything below sounds reasonable. Lead with Essential and everything above sounds like a rip-off.
- Say a $30K number out loud without flinching. Practise it until it's boring.
- Most techs discount before the customer has even objected. Don't.

**Simulation: 🏚️ The $28K re-pipe.** A 1960s home with galvanised pipe, three leaks this year and low pressure. A couple; one is keen, the other thinks 'just patch the leaks'.
Strong moves: Walk through what failure looks like → Present Elite first, then narrow to the two that fit → State the price and hold the silence

**Field sign-off** (signed off by: Harriet (Sales Manager); William as backup)
- [ ] Two proposals over $10,000 presented top-option-first, with written findings and evidence

### 6. Finance: Seed It Three Times `x6`
**Goal:** Make finance standard on every big job, so the big number feels reachable.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 'Selling the Big Job' ch. 4, 6, 9–11, Oct 2026 Masterclass point 4
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 sessions (industry figures in them are US data: correlation, not guarantees)

**Lessons**
1. Touchpoint 1, during the inspection before any price: 'Whatever we find, we've got ways to make it easy, including spreading it out if you'd ever want'
2. Touchpoint 2, while walking through options, tied to Elite: 'For the bigger one, some customers just pay it weekly rather than all at once'
3. Touchpoint 3, after the price and the silence: 'Like I mentioned, I can turn that into a weekly figure in about two minutes'
4. Who wants finance: cash-is-king (keeps money invested), cashflow smoothers, and the budget-constrained. Finance means savvy, not broke
5. Offer it to everyone with a flattering reason. Offered upfront it's a feature; offered after 'too expensive' it's a lifeline
6. The HomePro flow: Option → Apply → Approved → Book → Finish. HomePro sits outside ServiceTitan, so the proposal shows the full scope and real price and never mentions finance
7. Quoting weekly figures: always use HomePro's real numbers, never an illustration

**Key points to teach**
- Same line, same tone, to every customer on every big job. A throwaway line, never a pitch.
- Benchmark: if fewer than 40% of big jobs are financed, it isn't being offered consistently.
- Lead with the solution; finance removes the barrier. It's the 'how', not the 'what'.

**Simulation: 💳 The quiet flinch.** You've stated $14,800 for the Premium option. They go silent and look at each other.
Strong moves: Hold the silence → Bring finance home as something they've already heard ('like I mentioned…') → Offer the weekly figure from HomePro, not a discount

### 7. The Customer Who Says They Can't Afford It (and Other Hard Objections) `x7`
**Goal:** Remove the barrier, never drop the price.
*Reuse content from:* Oct 2026 Masterclass points 2, 7, 8 & 10, sales/sa8
*Draws on:* Proximity Oct 2026 Masterclass; Jeb Blount (Objections): a short pause and acknowledgement, a reframe, then ask for the next step; Sandler: pain, budget, decision qualification before presenting; NEPQ: questions that let the customer state the consequences themselves

**Lessons**
1. 'I can't afford it' almost never means they can't pay: they don't see the value yet, they're scared of the number, or nobody gave them a way to say yes
2. Separate can't from won't: 'Totally fair. Can I ask what feels like too much about it?'
3. Feel, felt, found: disagree without arguing
4. Never discount for free: if the price comes down, scope comes out. 'I can't do that price on the full job. If we do just the urgent part today, here's where we land'
5. 'You're 30% dearer': compare scope line by line against price-led competitors
6. 'We need to think about it' / 'talk to my partner': agree the follow-up time and what they'll decide
7. 'Can you sharpen your pencil?' from builders and property managers
8. Selling is service: letting a family walk away with a dodgy patch because we were too soft to have the money conversation is not kindness

**Key points to teach**
- The objection turnaround: pause and acknowledge, ask one clarifying question, reframe (value, consequence or a lower tier), then ask for the next step. One at a time.
- Price only comes down when scope comes out. Otherwise they learn our first number was fake.
- You can't discount, and only Harriet or Sadie can change a price. Never say 'let me see what the office can do'. Step down an option instead.
- A respectful 'not yet' with a booked follow-up is a win, not a loss.

**Simulation: 🚪 Three doors.** Door 1: you say the price and they go silent. Door 2: 'Can you do it cheaper?' on the Premium option. Door 3: 'Money's tight right now, we'll have to think about it.'
Strong moves: Use the lines from the session word for word → Clarify can't vs won't → Remove scope or offer finance, never a free discount
