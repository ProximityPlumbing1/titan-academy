## Running a preventive visit

A preventive visit isn't a call-out. Nothing's broken. The customer has asked us to check their home so nothing goes wrong. **Preventive visits are reassuring first.**

Run it to the same visit standard as any job: on time, shoe covers on, drop sheets down. Then:

1. **Tell them what you'll check** and roughly how long it'll take.
2. **Run the whole-home health check**: pressure and the PRV, flexi hoses, isolation valves, hot water age, the stop tap, a meter test, the gully and visible leaks.
3. **Photograph and video as you go**, the good as well as the bad.
4. **Sort what you found** into what needs attention now, what to keep an eye on, and what's fine.
5. **Walk them through it**, starting with the good news.

If everything's in good shape, that's a good result. **Say so clearly.** Don't keep hunting for something to sell, and never inflate a finding. If you do find something, explain it calmly and give them the options, with no pressure.

Before you leave, make sure they know where the stop tap is and how to turn it off.

> **On the job:** A preventive visit in Beecroft finds everything in good order, except a hot water unit that's about nine years old. You lead with the good news, then explain the age flag calmly: nothing's wrong today, but it's worth planning for. She thanks you for not making it sound like a crisis.

## Notes for the Preventive Maintenance report

Your notes become the customer's Preventive Maintenance report. The customer reads it, and so might anyone they share it with, so write it to stand on its own.

Use the standard note structure:

1. **Why called**: the scheduled preventive visit, plus anything the customer mentioned.
2. **What I found**: each item from the health check, good and bad.
3. **What I did**: anything you adjusted, repaired or tested.
4. **What I recommend**: what needs attention now and what to keep an eye on.

The rules:

- **Number your photos and refer to them**, for example "photo 4: flexi hose under the laundry tub, corrosion on the braid".
- **Record the "all good" items too.** "Isolation valves all operating" belongs in the report.
- **Record observations, not legal opinions.** Never write "illegal" or "non-compliant" in customer-facing notes.
- **Flag anything urgent** with the "Needs attention now" flag, so the office calls the same day.
- **Tag what drives follow-up**, such as hot-water-8yr+, ageing-flexi-hose or PRV-monitor.

> **On the job:** In Cherrybrook, one note says "HWS old, bit of rust." Another says "Electric storage hot water unit, about nine years old per the data plate (photo 6). Light rust at the base (photo 7). No leaks. Tagged hot-water-8yr+." The second one is a report. The first one is a guess.

## Offering the plan

**Offer the Care Plan on every completed job.** It's one of the weekly tracked habits, alongside four options on every job and Elite presented first.

Every completed job means every one: not just the big ones, and not just customers who "look like" members. Offered to some people and not others, it becomes a judgement about them. Offered to everyone, it's simply how we do things.

- **Offer it at the end**, during the walk-through, once the work is done and the customer is happy. It fits naturally after you've explained the warranty.
- **Keep it short and the same every time.** A throwaway line, never a pitch.
- **Link it to what you found** where that's genuine, like an ageing hot water unit.
- **A "no" is fine.** Thank them and move on. The habit is the offer, not the sale.
