## Looking for extra work on every job: hot water age, flexi hoses, PRVs, filtration, valves

You're in more homes than anyone else in the business, and you see things the office never will. **Look for genuine extra work on every job.**

While you work, keep an eye on:

- **Hot water age.** Check the date on the unit. Units around 8 years or older get flagged for follow-up.
- **Flexi hoses.** Perished, kinked, rusted or frayed hoses under sinks and toilets.
- **PRVs.** Is the mains pressure high? Is there a pressure-limiting valve, and is it working?
- **Filtration.** Has the customer mentioned taste, smell or sediment in their water?
- **Valves.** Missing or seized isolation valves, or a stop tap nobody can find.

Genuine means genuine. If you wouldn't recommend it in your own home, don't raise it. **Never inflate a finding.** One exaggerated problem costs more trust than the extra work is worth, and calm, honest advice is what earns the reviews.

This isn't pushing. It's the customer hearing about the next problem from you, before it becomes an emergency call.

> **On the job:** You're replacing a toilet suite in Hurstville. Under the basin, the flexi hoses are rusty and one is kinked. Outside, the hot water unit's label says it was made 11 years ago. Nobody asked you to look. What do you do with what you've found, and when do you bring it up?

Spotting it is half the job. **Photograph it, explain it, and quote it or raise it.**

1. **Photograph it.** Close up and wide, with the rust, the kink or the date label clearly visible. Showing beats telling.
2. **Explain it** in plain English, at the right moment. That's after you've done the job well, often during the walk-through, never as a rushed mention at the door on the way out. Say what it is, what it means for them, and what's likely to happen if it's left.

## Raising issues with the Install Manager straight away: scope, parts, access, customer

**Tell the Install Manager about problems immediately, not at the end of the day.** Until one is hired, that's Charlotte (Operations Manager, interim).

The four things to raise straight away:

- **Scope**: the site doesn't match the quote, or the job needs something that isn't in it.
- **Parts**: something's missing, wrong or damaged.
- **Access**: you can't get in, or you can't reach where the work is.
- **Customer**: they're unhappy, they're expecting something different, or they aren't the person who approved the job.

Why straight away? A problem raised at 9am can often still be fixed today: a part sent out, the customer called, the schedule moved. The same problem raised at 4pm usually means a lost day and a return trip.

Urgent problems get a call, not a message. If it changes the schedule, keep dispatch (Kim) in the loop too. On a planned job a Service Tech sold, that Service Tech captains the job, so let them know as well.

> **On the job:** At the start of a job in Northmead, you unbox the new vanity and it's the wrong width for the space. Call the Install Manager now. By the end-of-day brief, it's too late to fix today.

## Pacing bigger jobs to finish on time and to standard

Bigger installs are booked for a set period: a full day, two days, a week. **Pace the work so it finishes on time and to standard.** Not early with corners cut, and not late with excuses.

- **Know the booked time** before you start, and break the job into stages: what's done by lunch, by the end of today, by the end of tomorrow.
- **Check your progress against it** at each stage. If you're behind, you find out early enough to do something about it.
- **Work in the right order** so nothing gets done twice. Rework is the fastest way to blow the hours.
- **Have the parts for each stage ready** before you need them.
- **Flag it early** if the job won't finish in the booked time. Call the Install Manager the moment you know, not on the last afternoon.
- **Don't rush to finish early.** Speed without quality is failure, because the lifetime labour warranty brings the job back.

Your end-of-day brief records what's done and what's outstanding, so everyone can see how the job is tracking.

For this module's sign-off, you finish a multi-day job within the booked time.

> **On the job:** You're on a three-day re-pipe in Lindfield. You planned to finish the subfloor run by the end of day one, but at lunch you're well behind because the old pipe is fighting you. That's the moment to rethink the plan and call the Install Manager, not the afternoon of day three.

## Keeping the customer updated on multi-day jobs

On a multi-day job, the customer is living around your work. They don't need every detail, but they should never have to wonder what's happening.

- **Start of each day**: what you're doing today, roughly when the water or gas will be off, and anything they need to move or keep clear.
- **During the day**: tell them before anything changes, like a longer shut-off or a noisy stage.
- **End of each day**: what's done, what's left, and what they'll have tonight: water, a working toilet, hot water. Leave the site safe, tidy and secure.
- **If the finish date changes**, they hear it early from you or the Install Manager, never as a surprise on the last day.

A customer who's kept in the loop forgives the odd hiccup. A customer left wondering starts ringing the office, and the happy call goes very differently.

> **Say it like this:** "Quick update before I go. The new pipework's in and tested, and your water's back on for tonight. Tomorrow I'll be in the ceiling, and the water will be off again for a couple of hours in the morning. I'll knock before I turn it off."
