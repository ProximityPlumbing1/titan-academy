## Stop: don't carry on with work that isn't in the approved quote

The customer agreed to a fixed price for a fixed scope. The moment you find work that isn't in the approved quote, **stop.**

Stopping doesn't mean downing tools and walking off. It means you don't do the extra work, or anything that commits the customer to it, until it's documented, priced and approved. If something is unsafe, make it safe first: isolate the water or gas. Then stop.

Why it matters:

- **It breaks the no-hidden-costs promise.** The customer finds out about extra work on the invoice, or never.
- **It's the biggest silent profit leak on installs.** Work that's done and never priced is paid for by the business, in hours and materials.
- **It costs you.** Unapproved hours still count against the estimate, so your efficiency takes the hit.

The order, every time:

**Stop → document → explain and re-price → signed approval → continue.**

**Unsure? Call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before you continue.**

> **On the job:** Halfway through a bathroom fit-off in Drummoyne, you find corroded galvanised pipe behind the wall that isn't in the scope. The customer says, "Just do it, we'll sort it out later." It would only take an hour or so. What do you do?

## Document it: photos or video and a note in ServiceTitan

Before you say a word to the customer, document what you've found.

- **Photos or video** of the problem: close up, and wide enough to show where it is. If it's inside a wall or under a floor, show how you found it.
- **A note in ServiceTitan**: what you found, where it is, why it isn't in the scope, and what it needs.
- **Observations, not legal opinions.** Never write "illegal" or "non-compliant" in customer-facing notes (f5).

Good documentation does three jobs:

- it **shows the customer** what you found, because showing beats telling
- it **protects you** if anyone asks later why the price changed
- it **tells the Install Manager and whoever sold the job** what the scope missed, so the next scope is better

Scope changes captured and approved are one of the things installers are measured on. A change that isn't documented looks the same as one that never happened.

> **On the job:** Back in Drummoyne, you take a short video along the corroded section, then a close-up of the worst joint. Your note reads: "Corroded galvanised pipe behind the shower wall, not in scope. Recommend replacing this section before fit-off. Photos 4 to 6."

## Explain it to the customer and give a new price from the pricebook

Now show the customer. Use your photos or video and explain in plain English: what you found, what it means for them, and what's likely to happen if it's left. Calm and honest. Never inflate it.

Then **give them a new price from the pricebook.** Not a guess. Not "about a grand". Not "depends how it goes". The customer sees exactly what the change costs before they decide.

- **You can't discount it** and you can't do it for free. Only Harriet (Sales Manager) or Sadie (Office Manager) can change a price.
- **No side deals or cash prices.** Every change goes through ServiceTitan at the pricebook price.
- **Not sure which pricebook lines fit?** Call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before you give a number.
- **If they say no**, record it in the notes with your photos, and call the Install Manager about finishing the approved work.

> **Say it like this:** "I've found something behind the wall that wasn't in the quote. Let me show you. I'll price it from our pricebook now, so you can see exactly what it costs before you decide."

## Get signed approval before continuing

"Yeah, go ahead" isn't approval. **Signed approval in ServiceTitan before you continue** is what keeps the fixed-price promise honest.

1. Add the change to the job in ServiceTitan at its pricebook price.
2. Have the customer sign it on your device.
3. Only then pick the tools back up.
4. Let the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) know about the change, and whether it affects the finish time.

Not "I'll text the office tonight". Not "I'll add it to the invoice at the end". Both leave the customer with a surprise and you with no proof they agreed.

**Check who can approve.** On a tenant job, the property manager approves the work, not the tenant. Never go past a property manager's approval limit without written approval. If the person who can approve isn't there, carry on only with the approved work and call the Install Manager.

For this module's sign-off, you correctly process a scope change on a live job.

> **On the job:** In a Parramatta rental, the tenant tells you to "just fix it, the landlord won't mind". The tenant can't approve it. You document it, call the Install Manager, and carry on with only the approved work until the property manager signs.
