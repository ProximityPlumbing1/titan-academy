## Getting the plan signed in ServiceTitan

A "yes" at the kitchen table isn't approval yet. **The plan is approved when the customer signs it in ServiceTitan.**

The signed plan is what the customer is paying for and what the installer will build. If it isn't signed in ServiceTitan, it didn't happen.

Before they sign, check:

- the option they chose is the one that's selected
- the scope says what's included and what isn't
- every line comes from the pricebook, with nothing hand-typed
- they've read it, and their questions are answered

If they change their mind at the table, for example by dropping a line to bring the price down, update the estimate before they sign. Never get one thing signed and promise to "sort out the rest later".

> **On the job:** In Dundas, a customer says, "Yep, let's do Premium. Just book it in and I'll sign when your installer turns up." It's your last job and you're keen to get going. What do you do, and what could go wrong if you leave without a signature?

> **Say it like this:** "Great choice. Let's get it signed now, so I can lock in your date and get your parts ordered."

**No 50% deposit, no scheduling and no materials ordered.** The job doesn't get booked and nothing gets ordered until the deposit is in, so don't promise an install date before then. The deposit means the customer has committed before we commit an installer's day and buy materials for their job.

## Balance on completion

The second half is paid **on completion**: when the work is finished, tested, walked through with the customer and signed off in ServiceTitan.

On installs, the **Install Manager (Charlotte, interim)** owns payment collection and the customer's happiness. The installer collects the balance on site before leaving. Anything overdue goes to Chloe, who chases unpaid invoices, and Finance (Carla) takes over from there.

Your part starts at the kitchen table. A balance is easy to collect when the customer has known about it from the start:

- tell them the balance is due on the day the job is finished
- tell them what happens next, and who they'll hear from
- put anything they tell you about payment in the customer notes

**No hidden costs** means the balance is the signed price less the deposit. The only thing that changes it is extra work the customer has approved and signed for.

If you sold a planned job, you're its job captain. The Install Manager still owns the payment, but the customer will remember what you told them at signing.

> **On the job:** You sell a planned re-pipe in Gladesville and take the deposit. On the last day, the customer is surprised when the installer asks for the balance: "I thought you'd send an invoice." One sentence from you at signing would have saved that conversation.

## The handover: scope, materials, photos, access, hours, customer notes

The handover is everything dispatch and the installer need to deliver what you sold. **The handover is your promise to the installer and the customer. Missing information costs everyone time.**

Every handover has six parts:

- **Scope:** what's included and what isn't, in plain English.
- **Materials:** everything the job needs, including specials that must be ordered.
- **Photos and video:** numbered and saved to the job, showing what you found and where the work goes.
- **Access:** parking, keys, gates, pets, a strata building, or a tenant who gives access.
- **Hours:** realistic estimated hours. They set the installer's day and their efficiency pay.
- **Customer notes:** what matters to them, what they're expecting on the day, and how they like to be contacted.

The test: **could the installer do this job without ever calling you?** If not, it isn't finished.

Kim (residential dispatch) accepts handovers and books the job. A handover with something missing comes back to you, and the job waits. Your sign-off for this module is three handovers accepted by Kim without missing information.

> **On the job:** Your handover for a hot water upgrade in St Ives says "replace unit, all in photos". The installer arrives to a locked side gate, a dog in the yard, and no idea the owner wanted the new unit further from the bedroom window. Two lines in the handover would have covered all three.

## When the installer finds something different

Sometimes the installer opens a wall or lifts a floor and finds something nobody could see when you quoted. The rule on site doesn't change:

1. **Stop.** No work that isn't in the signed scope.
2. **Document it** with photos or video and a note in ServiceTitan.
3. **Explain it** to the customer and give a new price from the pricebook.
4. **Get signed approval** before continuing.

That's what keeps "no hidden costs" true. The customer never pays for work they didn't approve, and nobody does extra work quietly for free.

If you sold the job, you're its job captain, so questions about what you sold come to you. Answer them quickly: the installer is on the clock. The Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) handles the customer, the day and the payment, and the installer calls her if unsure whether to continue. Nobody discounts to smooth it over. Only Harriet or Sadie can change a price.

Then learn from it. If you could have found it, inspect further next time. If it couldn't be seen, say so in the scope: "depends on what's found once the wall is opened".

> **On the job:** You sold a bathroom refresh in Epping. Halfway through, the installer calls: the pipe behind the old vanity is corroded galvanised steel, and it isn't in the scope. You confirm what was sold and what your photos showed. The installer photographs it, gives the customer a new price from the pricebook and gets signed approval before going any further.
