## Videos and before-and-after photos on every job, saved to the job

**Every job gets video and before-and-after photos, saved to the job in ServiceTitan.** Not just the big ones, and not left in your phone's camera roll.

They protect you, they prove the work, and they're how the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) signs off your work without standing in the room.

Work to the same shot list every time:

- **Before:** a wide shot of the area, close-ups of what you'll work on, and any existing damage.
- **During:** key stages, especially anything that will be hidden once it's finished, like pipework behind a wall or under a floor.
- **After:** the finished work, the tidy site and any test results.
- **Video:** a short walk-through before you start, and again once it's finished and working.

Take the before shots before a single tool comes out. Once you've started, you can never prove what was there.

Number your photos and refer to them in your notes, for example "photo 4: existing crack in tile beside vanity".

> **On the job:** You're about to pull out an old vanity in Concord when you notice a cracked floor tile right beside it. Nobody has mentioned it. What do you do before you touch anything, and what do you say to the customer?

> **Say it like this:** "Just so you know, this tile was already cracked before I started. I've taken a photo so it's on the record."

## Picking up parts: plan it so it doesn't hold up the job, and note what was collected for which job

**Parts pickups are planned, not mid-job surprises, and every pickup is noted against the job.**

Plan them the day before, when you check tomorrow's jobs:

- what's already on the van, and what isn't
- which specials need ordering or collecting, and when they'll be ready
- whether the pickup fits on the way, before the job starts

If a pickup will change when you arrive, tell dispatch (Kim) before it does, and call the customer.

Then note every pickup against the job it's for in ServiceTitan: what you collected, and for which job. If you collect parts for two jobs in one trip, put each part against the right job. Parts that aren't recorded against a job are materials nobody billed, and unbilled materials come straight out of the profit.

A mid-job run still happens sometimes. When it does, tell the customer, tell dispatch and note it. Then ask yourself what you'd have needed to check the day before to avoid it.

> **On the job:** In one stop, you collect a tempering valve for tomorrow's job in Seven Hills and a mixer for Thursday's job in Rouse Hill. Note each part against its own job before you drive off, not on Friday when you've forgotten which was which.

## End-of-day job briefs: what's done, what's outstanding, any issues

**The end-of-day brief means anyone can pick the job up tomorrow.** If you're sick, held up or sent somewhere else, another installer, dispatch or the Install Manager can see exactly where the job is at.

Every brief covers three things:

- **What's done:** the work completed today, and what's been tested.
- **What's outstanding:** what's left, and anything waiting on parts, another trade or the customer.
- **Any issues:** problems, scope questions, customer concerns, access changes.

Keep it short, factual and specific. "Got most of it done" isn't a brief. Refer to your numbered photos so the reader can see what you mean.

> **On the job:** Day one of a two-day re-pipe in Hunters Hill ends with the water back on but two isolation valves still to fit. Overnight, you're moved to another job. Because your brief says exactly what's left and where the parts are, the installer who takes over doesn't lose an hour working it out.

## Statuses, forms and timesheets in real time

**Real-time updates, never at the end of the week.** Statuses, forms and timesheets are updated as you go.

The habit is simple: tools out, timer on; tools away, timer off. Update your status when you're on your way, when you start and when you finish.

Real time matters because:

- **Your pay is measured from it.** The gap between estimated and actual hours only counts if your time is recorded accurately. A timesheet back-filled from memory hides the hour you saved.
- **Dispatch runs the day from it.** Kim can only plan around what the board shows.
- **Forms are right when they're fresh.** Checklists and test results filled in on site are accurate. Filled in on Friday, they're guesses.

If it isn't in ServiceTitan, it didn't happen. That includes your time.

> **On the job:** It's Friday afternoon and you're catching up a week of timesheets from memory. You beat the estimate on Tuesday's re-pipe in Drummoyne, but now you can't remember by how much, so you round it to the estimate. That saved hour, and the $50 of value with it, has just disappeared.

## Keeping dispatch (Kim) and the Install Manager updated

Two people need to know where your jobs are at, and they need different things.

**Kim (residential dispatch)** runs the board. Keep her updated on:

- when you're on site, running over, finished or blocked
- pickups or delays that change when you'll reach the next job

**The Install Manager (Charlotte, interim)** owns delivery, the customer's happiness and payment. Tell her straight away, not at the end of the day, about:

- a site that doesn't match the scope
- missing or wrong parts
- access problems
- an unhappy customer, or a payment issue

Pick the right channel. Call when it's urgent or needs a conversation, like a job that's going to overrun. A message or a status update is fine for routine things. Never bury an urgent problem in a text.

The goal is no surprises. Dispatch and the Install Manager should hear about a problem from you, early, while there's still time to fix it.

> **Say it like this:** "Hi Kim, it's [your name]. The bathroom in Ryde is going to run about an hour over. The customer knows, and I'll call my next customer when I'm on my way."
