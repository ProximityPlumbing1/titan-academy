## How a customer moves through Proximity

Most jobs pass through several people. Here's the usual path:

1. **The job is booked.** Kim (residential dispatch) or Ruby (commercial dispatch) books it.
2. **A Service Tech visits.** Most customers meet Proximity this way. The tech diagnoses, fixes what they called about, checks the rest of the system and offers the options.
3. **Done today, or planned.** Work done on the spot is paid on completion. Bigger work becomes a plan, quoted by the Service Tech or a Sales Tech.
4. **The plan is approved and the 50% deposit paid.** Then it's scheduled, the materials are ordered and it's handed over to install.
5. **The installer does the work**, then walks the customer through it, explains the warranty, collects the balance and asks for a review.
6. **Chloe makes a happy call** to check how it went.
7. **If anything isn't right**, we come back under the warranty or a same-day return.

**Each role depends on the one before it.** A bad scope costs the installer time and the business its profit. A missing access note costs the morning. A rushed install becomes a return.

The customer only sees one company, and they judge it by the weakest step.

> **On the job:** A customer in Pennant Hills calls with no hot water. Follow her through: who books it, who diagnoses it, who sells the replacement, who installs it, who collects the balance and who calls her afterwards. Now pick one step and imagine it going wrong. Who feels it next?

## Service Techs sell on the spot; Sales Techs quote larger and planned work

There are two kinds of selling at Proximity, and both follow the same rules.

**Service Techs sell on the spot.** You diagnose, fix what they called about, and present the options at the kitchen table. If the customer chooses work that can be done today, you do it today. If it's bigger, you book it as a planned job with a 50% deposit and a proper handover.

**Sales Techs quote larger and planned work.** They're our most experienced sellers. They quote, sell and hand over. [CONFIRM: which jobs go to a Sales Tech rather than being sold by the Service Tech]

**Install Techs deliver the planned work**, and raise any extra work they spot along the way.

Whoever is selling, the rules don't change:

- **Four options on every job**: Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite, with Elite presented first.
- **Never quote one job.** Bundle from what you actually found.
- **Every line from the pricebook**, with no bundle discounts.
- **No discounting.** Only Harriet (Sales Manager) or Sadie (Office Manager) can change a price.

Larger strata work goes to Bella (Business Partnerships), and commercial work goes to Alex (Commercial Manager).

> **On the job:** In Maroubra, you fix the dripping kitchen tap the customer called about. Your health check finds perished flexi hoses right through the house. You build four options, Elite first, and she picks Premium. You do it today. That's selling on the spot: found, shown, priced from the pricebook, done.

## Approved plan → 50% deposit → scheduled → materials ordered → Install

Planned work moves in a fixed order, and **no step is skipped.**

1. **Approved plan.** The customer signs the plan in ServiceTitan.
2. **50% deposit.** It's collected when the plan is approved. Sales Techs collect it at signing. [CONFIRM: whether the 50% rule applies to every planned job, including ones a Service Tech books]
3. **Scheduled.** The job is booked with Kim (residential dispatch). Charlotte (Install Manager, interim) accepts handovers with Kim.
4. **Materials ordered.** Parts and specials are ordered so they're ready on the day.
5. **Install.** The installer confirms the job the day before and reads the handover.

**No deposit, no scheduling and no materials ordered.** Order materials before the deposit is paid, and the business can end up paying for parts on a job that never goes ahead.

The handover is the link between selling and installing. It has to include the scope (what's in and what's not), materials and specials, photos and video, access, estimated hours, and customer notes and expectations.

The test: **could the installer do this job without calling the person who sold it?** If not, the handover isn't finished.

> **On the job:** A re-pipe in Drummoyne is sold with hours that assume easy access, but the subfloor is low and tight. The installer runs hours over. The customer still pays the fixed price. The business pays for the extra hours, and the installer loses the efficiency they'd have earned.

## Who owns a sold job: Sales Techs sell and hand over; the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) owns delivery, customer happiness and payment collection. For planned jobs a Service Tech sells, the Service Tech captains the job

Once a job is sold, everyone needs to know who owns it.

- **Sales Techs only sell.** Their job ends with a complete handover. After that, the job belongs to the Install Manager.
- **The Install Manager owns delivery, the customer's happiness and payment collection.** The installer collects the balance on site, and anything overdue goes to Chloe.
- **Service Techs captain the planned jobs they sell.** That means the handover, the installer's questions and the finish, with the Install Manager owning payment and the customer's happiness.

There's no permanent Install Manager yet. **Charlotte (Operations Manager) is covering the role** until one is hired. The permanent Install Manager will manage the Service Techs too.

For installers, this means one thing above all: **raise issues with the Install Manager straight away.** Scope, parts, access or the customer: call Charlotte as soon as you know, not at the end of the day. Don't improvise, and don't wait for it to fix itself.

If a Service Tech sold the job, they can answer questions about what was sold. Problems still go to the Install Manager.

> **On the job:** You arrive at an install in Concord and the special valve on the handover hasn't been delivered. You don't sit in the van or start without it. You call Charlotte straight away, while there's still time to sort it out.

## Any tech → raising extra work they've spotted

You're in more homes than anyone in the office. You see the ageing hot water unit and the perished hoses that nobody else will.

**Extra work is raised in ServiceTitan, with photos and notes.** Never just a verbal "mention it to the office". A mention gets forgotten. A lead in ServiceTitan gets quoted and followed up.

A good raise includes:

- **What you found and where**, with photos and video.
- **Why it matters** to the customer.
- **What they said**, and how keen they seemed.

Only raise genuine needs. If you wouldn't recommend it to your own family, don't raise it. Never invent or exaggerate a problem.

Raise it after you've done good work, and never let it overshadow the job they called about.

Install Techs earn **1%** on extra work they raise that's sold and completed. [CONFIRM: whether installers quote extra work themselves from templates or raise it for a Service or Sales Tech]

If the extra work affects the install you're doing right now, that's a change to scope. Stop, document it, and call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before you carry on.

Your sign-off for this module is correctly raising extra work or an issue in ServiceTitan.

> **On the job:** You're replacing a hot water unit in Wahroonga and notice the flexi hoses under the kitchen sink are corroded. You photograph them, show the customer, and raise it in ServiceTitan with the photos and a note. If it's sold and completed, the 1% is yours.

## Warranty returns and same-day returns

Our promise includes a **lifetime labour warranty** [CONFIRM: approved wording, including what's excluded] and a **same-day return** if something isn't right [CONFIRM: exactly what this covers]. Sometimes that means going back.

Warranty and same-day-return jobs are **linked to the original job** in ServiceTitan. That way anyone can see what was done the first time, and the business can learn from it.

**Handle every return graciously.** The customer is already disappointed. They don't want excuses. They want it fixed, quickly and politely.

- Fix it properly, first time.
- Never blame another tech, dispatch or the office.
- Note what you found, and what caused it if you can tell.

Returns are expensive. Every return trip is unpaid time and lost capacity, it costs the business its profit on the original job, and it dents the customer's trust. For an installer, it wipes out the time saved on the job.

One return now and then happens to everyone. **A lot of them signals a quality problem**: with a tech, a product, a job type or the way jobs are scoped. That's why they're tracked.

> **On the job:** You're sent back to a Mosman home where a mixer another tech fitted last month is dripping. You don't say "whoever did this...". You apologise for the hassle, fix it, and note what you found so it can be learned from.
