## Site safety and the right PPE for the job

Everything from Safety, Licensing & Compliance in Field Foundations applies on every install: the site risk check and SWMS before you start, and **anyone can stop a job if it's unsafe**. Nobody is ever criticised for it.

Installs add their own hazards, because you're opening things up:

- **Demolition:** hidden pipes and cables, sharp edges, dust, and material that can fall.
- **Wall cavities:** live cables, gas and water pipes, and old building materials you can't see until you cut.
- **Roof spaces:** heat, poor light, fragile ceilings, cables across the joists, and insulation fibres.

**Wear the right PPE for the job, on every job.** Not only on the big ones, and not only when the customer is watching. Match it to the task: eye protection when cutting or drilling, hearing protection with demolition tools, a suitable mask for dust and fibres, gloves for sharp edges and sewage, and proper boots on site.

Before you cut or drill, find out what's behind the wall. Treat every cable as live.

> **On the job:** You need to run new pipe through the roof space of an older home in Strathfield on a hot afternoon. The light's poor, the ceiling is old plaster and there are cables across the joists. What do you check and organise before you go up?

## Isolating water or gas before starting

**Isolate water or gas before starting. Every time.** Then test that it's actually off. No exceptions, however small the job.

1. **Find the right isolation point:** the isolation valve, the stop tap or the gas valve for what you're working on.
2. **Turn it off**, and tell the customer what's off and roughly for how long.
3. **Test it's off** before you cut or undo anything. For water, open the nearest tap. For gas, follow the testing procedure for your licence. [VERIFY: current NSW gas isolation and testing requirements]
4. **Keep it off.** Make sure the customer, and anyone else on site, knows not to turn it back on.
5. **Restore and test** when you've finished, and check for leaks before you leave.

If a valve won't fully close, don't work around it. Go back to the next isolation point, usually the main, and test again.

**Only do work your licence covers.** Gas work is only for licensed gasfitters, and apprentices and supervised workers have a named supervisor on every job. [CONFIRM: which techs hold gas licences]

> **On the job:** On a bathroom job in Epping, you close the isolation valve under the basin but water keeps trickling from the old line. The valve is seized part-open. You turn off the main at the stop tap, test at the nearest tap, and let the customer know the whole house is off for a while.

## Asbestos and hazard awareness: stop and ask if unsure

Installs mean opening walls and floors in older Sydney homes. That's where you'll meet materials that may contain asbestos, such as fibro sheeting, AC (asbestos cement) pipe and old vinyl flooring.

**You can't tell by looking. If you're unsure, stop and ask.**

If you suspect asbestos:

1. **Stop.** Never cut, drill, break, sand or move it.
2. **Photograph it** and note where it is.
3. **Flag it in ServiceTitan.**
4. **Call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) or the office.**
5. **Keep people away** from the area until you have an answer.

Never bag it or remove it yourself. The Install Manager or the office works out the next step with the customer. [VERIFY: current SafeWork NSW requirements for testing and removing suspected asbestos]

The same rule covers any hazard you didn't expect: a cable where it shouldn't be, a smell of gas, sewage in a cavity, or a ceiling that won't take your weight. Stop, make it safe, and ask.

> **On the job:** In an old laundry in Ermington, you pull off the skirting and find grey sheeting behind the tub. The job is running late, and cutting through would take two minutes. You stop, photograph it, flag it and call the Install Manager.

> **Say it like this:** "I've found some old sheeting behind the tub that might contain asbestos. I'm not going to touch it until it's checked. I'm calling the office now so we can work out the next step with you."

## Compliance paperwork and certificates lodged on time [VERIFY]

**Certificates lodged on time are part of finishing the job.** They're not admin for later, and they're not the office's problem.

Plumbing, drainage and gas work comes with paperwork that shows it meets the rules. Which work needs a certificate, how it's lodged and the deadline are set by the regulator, so check the current process rather than going from memory. [VERIFY: current NSW Fair Trading process for certificates of compliance, including which work needs one and the lodging deadline]

The principles don't change:

- **Only certify work your licence covers.** If you're supervised, check with your named supervisor before anything is lodged. [VERIFY: who signs and lodges for work done under supervision]
- **Do it while the details are in front of you**, on site, as part of finishing.
- **Attach a copy to the job in ServiceTitan**, so the record is complete and anyone can find it.
- **Record what you see, not legal opinions.** If you find old work that looks wrong, describe it and photograph it. Never write "illegal" or "non-compliant" in customer-facing notes.

Your sign-off for this module is lodging compliance paperwork correctly on a live job, checked by a lead installer.

> **On the job:** You finish a hot water replacement in Castle Hill late in the afternoon and tell yourself you'll do the certificate on the weekend. By then you've done four more jobs and the details are a blur. Do it as part of finishing the job, while everything is in front of you.
