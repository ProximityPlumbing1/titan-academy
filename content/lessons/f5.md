## The standard: videos plus before-and-after photos on every job, saved to the job in ServiceTitan

**Every job gets before-and-after photos and video, from a tap washer to a full re-pipe. No exceptions.**

They do three things:

- **They protect you.** A photo taken before you start proves the crack, the stain or the leak was already there.
- **They prove the work.** Clear before-and-after shots show exactly what the customer paid for.
- **They feed the reports.** Your photos become the customer reports that win the next job.

Work to the same shot list every time:

- **Before:** a wide shot of the area, a close-up of the problem, and any existing damage nearby.
- **During:** anything that will be hidden once it's finished, like pipework behind a wall or under the floor.
- **After:** the finished work from the same angles as the before shots, plus the tidy site and any test results.
- **Video:** the problem in action (the drip, the noise, the slow drain) and the finished work running.

Take the before shots **before a single tool comes out**. Once you've started, you can't go back.

Save everything to the job in ServiceTitan, not just your camera roll. If it isn't in ServiceTitan, it didn't happen.

> **On the job:** You're swapping a vanity mixer in a Randwick unit, running behind, and the cabinet floor under the basin is already swollen from an old leak. Three weeks later the owner rings to say your work ruined the cabinet. Without a before photo, it's your word against theirs. What do you wish you'd done?

## The note structure: Why called → What I found → What I did → What I recommend

Photos show what happened. Notes explain it. Every job note follows the same four parts, in the same order:

1. **Why called:** what the customer said the problem was, in their words.
2. **What I found:** what you saw and tested, including the things that are fine.
3. **What I did:** the work you completed and the key materials you used.
4. **What I recommend:** anything that still needs attention, and why.

**Number your photos and refer to them in your notes.** "Photo 3: corroded flexi hose under the kitchen sink" lets anyone reading the note find the problem in seconds.

Notes like "Fixed leak" or "All good" tell the next person nothing. Neither does a page of rambling. Aim for short, factual and specific: a note the office, the next tech, or you in six months can understand straight away.

Record what's fine too. "Isolation valves all working" is useful information, and it shows the customer you checked.

Your sign-off for this module is **three jobs in a row** where your notes, photos and video pass office review without being sent back.

> **On the job:** A dripping kitchen tap in Carlingford. Why called: tap dripping for two weeks. What I found: worn cartridge (photo 1); photo 3, corroded flexi hose under the kitchen sink; isolation valves working. What I did: replaced the cartridge and tested, no leaks (photo 4). What I recommend: replace all the flexi hoses under the sinks (photos 3 and 5).

## How your notes become customer reports (Preventive, Private, Real Estate, Strata, Leak Detection, Strata Quote)

Your notes and photos are the raw material for the reports we send customers: **Preventive Maintenance, Private, Real Estate, Strata, Leak Detection and Strata Quote.** They go to homeowners, real estate agents and property managers, and strata managers and committees.

Whoever writes the report wasn't on site. Anything that isn't in your notes and photos can't go in the report. [CONFIRM: report team workflow and turnaround]

A clear report wins work. A strata committee that can see the problem in a photo and read what it means in plain English can approve the job. An agent who gets a useful report sends the next one.

Because notes become customer-facing, **record observations, not legal opinions.** Never write "illegal" or "non-compliant" in customer-facing notes, and never write "dangerous" or "done by a cowboy". You're not the regulator, and a word like that in a report can cause real problems for the customer and for us.

Write what you see, and what it means:

- "Showing signs of age and worth a licensed assessment."
- "Worth addressing before it becomes a bigger issue."

If you believe something is genuinely unsafe, describe exactly what you saw, photograph it, and flag it to the office (next lesson). Let the office decide how it's reported.

> **Say it like this:** "Photo 6: the pipework under the house shows signs of age and corrosion, and is worth a licensed assessment."

## Urgent findings: the 'Needs attention now' flag

Most findings can wait for the report. Some can't. **If you find something the customer has to deal with today, flag it so the office calls the same day.**

Urgent means a real risk of damage or harm if it's left, like water coming through a ceiling or a sewer overflowing in the yard. It's usually something you can't fix on this visit, because it's outside the job or needs approval or a quote.

When you find one:

1. **Make it safe if you can.** Follow your safety training, and stop if you're unsure.
2. **Tell the customer or tenant on the spot**, calmly and in plain English.
3. **Photograph and video it**, and write it up in your notes.
4. **Flag it 'Needs attention now'** in ServiceTitan so the office calls the same day. [CONFIRM: how the 'Needs attention now' flag is set in ServiceTitan]

Never leave it for the next visit, tuck it at the bottom of the invoice, or decide it's not your job. If you saw it, it's your job to flag it.

Keep the flag for genuinely urgent findings. If everything is urgent, nothing is, and the office stops trusting the flag.

> **On the job:** You're at a rental in Merrylands on an agent's work order for a blocked toilet. In the laundry, the hot water unit is leaking from the base. It's well beyond the work order. You tell the tenant, photograph it, and flag it 'Needs attention now' so the office reaches the agent today, not next week.

## Tags that drive follow-up (e.g. hot-water-8yr+, ageing-flexi-hose, PRV-monitor)

Not every finding needs fixing today, but plenty will need fixing one day. **Tags turn what you saw today into a follow-up call later.**

Common examples:

- **hot-water-8yr+**: the hot water unit is around 8 years old or more.
- **ageing-flexi-hose**: the flexi hoses are showing their age but haven't failed yet.
- **PRV-monitor**: the mains pressure or pressure valve (PRV) is worth checking again.

Use the standard tags only, and don't make up your own. A tag the office doesn't recognise drives nothing. [CONFIRM: the standard tag list]

Tag accurately. The follow-up call will be built on your notes and photos, so tag only what you found and photographed. A wrong tag means an awkward call and a customer who wonders what else we got wrong.

A tag isn't a substitute for a quote. If the customer wants the work priced now, raise it in ServiceTitan as a lead or follow-up, with photos and notes. Tags are for the things they're not ready to deal with yet.

> **On the job:** In a Gladesville home, the hot water unit is working fine, but the label shows it was installed nine years ago. You photograph the label, note it, and tag it hot-water-8yr+. The customer isn't ready to replace it today. When the office follows up, they have your photo and your note in front of them.
