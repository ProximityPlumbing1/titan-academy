## The day before: confirm job details, scope and parts

A good install starts the afternoon before. **Confirm the job details, scope and parts the day before** so nothing holds the job up.

Open tomorrow's jobs in ServiceTitan and read the handover properly:

- What exactly was sold? What's included, and what isn't?
- What parts and specials does it need, and are they ordered and ready?
- Are there access notes, like parking, keys, a strata building or a tenant?
- How many hours has the job been booked for?

If anything is missing or doesn't make sense, raise it now, not at 7am in the customer's driveway. Call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) while there's still time to fix it.

> **On the job:** The handover for tomorrow's hot water job in Kellyville doesn't say whether it's gas or electric. Ten minutes on the phone the afternoon before saves an hour and a wasted trip in the morning.

## 7am readiness: rolling to the first job, fully stocked

**7am readiness means you're rolling to the first job at 7am, fully stocked.** It doesn't mean arriving at base at 7am, and it doesn't mean loading the van at 7am.

Do the slow stuff the night before:

- fuel up at the end of the day
- check van stock against tomorrow's jobs
- arrange any specials or supplier pickups ahead of time
- put every tool back where it lives

Every item you forget becomes a supply run mid-job. Every saved hour against the estimate is worth about $50 to you, and a supply run burns those hours fast.

> **On the job:** Two installers both start at 7am. One is backing out of the driveway with everything on board. The other is still at the supplier collecting a valve they knew about yesterday. Same day, very different pay.

## Calling the customer when you're on the way

Call the customer **when you're on the way**. It's a small thing that sets the tone for the whole day.

The call does three jobs:

- it tells them when you'll arrive, so they're ready and the driveway is clear
- it's a chance to confirm access, pets and where to park
- if you're running late, it turns a complaint into a courtesy

If something has changed, like you've been held up or the arrival window has moved, call. Don't text at the last minute and hope.

> **Say it like this:** "Hi, it's [your name] from Proximity Plumbing. I'm on my way for your hot water install and should be there in about 20 minutes. Is there anywhere you'd like me to park?"

## On time, in uniform, shoe covers on, drop sheets down, work area protected

The first 30 seconds set the tone. The standard is the same on every job, big or small:

- **On time**, or you've called ahead.
- **In uniform**, clean and recognisable.
- **Shoe covers on** before you step inside.
- **Drop sheets down** along your path and around the work area.
- **Work area protected**, so furniture, floors and finishes are covered before you start.

Customers judge the whole company by what they see in the first minute. A tech who protects their home before touching anything gets trust straight away. A tech who walks dirt through the hallway has lost it before the job starts.

> **On the job:** In a newly renovated home in Bella Vista, the owner watches you lay drop sheets from the front door to the laundry before you bring in a single tool. She relaxes. You've just made the rest of the day easier.

## Walking the customer through the scope and signed quote before starting

**Don't start until you've walked the customer through the approved scope and quote and confirmed it's signed.**

Take a few minutes at the start:

- go through what was sold, in plain English
- point out what's included and what isn't
- confirm the quote is signed in ServiceTitan
- tell them roughly how long it will take, and when water or gas will be off

This is where you catch surprises early. If the customer expected something that isn't in the scope, it's much easier to sort out now than at the end of the day.

**If the site doesn't match the scope, call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before starting.** Don't improvise.

> **Say it like this:** "Before I start, let me run through what we're doing today so we're on the same page. Here's what's included, and here's what isn't."
