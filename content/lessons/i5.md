## Types: electric storage, gas storage, continuous flow, heat pump [CONFIRM brands]

**Hot water is our biggest install category, and it peaks from June to August.** In a Sydney winter, a family without hot water wants it back today, especially with young kids or older people in the house.

You'll install four main types:

- **Electric storage:** a tank heated by an element. Check the power supply suits the new unit.
- **Gas storage:** a tank heated by a gas burner. It needs a gas connection and a flue.
- **Continuous flow:** heats water only when a tap is on, usually on gas. It needs enough gas supply and the right spot for the flue.
- **Heat pump:** a tank that draws heat from the air. It needs airflow, a spot where the fan noise won't bother anyone, and somewhere for the condensation to drain.

Install exactly what was sold: the make and model in the signed scope. [CONFIRM: brands in the pricebook] If the site doesn't suit the unit, call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before you start, not halfway through.

This module gets a refresher every April and May, before the winter peak.

## Removal, disposal and site protection

The old unit is the heaviest, messiest part of the job, so plan it before you start.

**Protect the site first.** Lay drop sheets along the path from the unit to the van, and cover anything you'll pass with a heavy, wet unit.

Then take it out safely:

1. **Isolate everything:** water, gas and power. Power is isolated by someone licensed to do it. [VERIFY: who can disconnect and reconnect the electrical supply to a water heater in NSW]
2. **Drain it carefully.** The water may still be hot, and old tanks hold sediment. Run it to a drain, not across the lawn or the driveway.
3. **Lift it safely.** Old units are heavy and awkward. Use a trolley, and get help for the big ones.
4. **Check for hazards.** Old flues and the sheeting around older units can contain asbestos. If you're unsure, stop and ask.

Take the old unit away with you. [CONFIRM: disposal and recycling process for old units]

Then clean the spot it came from: the sediment, the old fittings, and the packaging from the new unit. Leave it cleaner than you found it.

> **On the job:** In a Cherrybrook garage, the old electric unit sits beside the owner's car and a shelf of boxes. Before you drain anything, you ask the owner to move the car, lay drop sheets to the roller door and run the hose to a drain. Ten minutes of setup saves a damaged car and an unhappy customer.

## Valves: tempering, PTR, isolation, non-return, relief drains

Valves are where a hot water install is safe and compliant, or isn't. Know what each one does:

- **Tempering valve:** mixes hot and cold so the water reaching the bathroom is at a safe temperature. The next lesson covers temperature.
- **PTR (pressure and temperature relief) valve:** lets water out if the pressure or temperature in the tank gets too high. Never cap it, block it or leave it without a drain line.
- **Isolation valve:** lets the unit be shut off without turning off the whole house.
- **Non-return valve:** stops heated water flowing back into the cold supply.
- **Relief drains:** the drain lines from the relief valves must run to a safe discharge point. [VERIFY: approved discharge points under AS/NZS 3500.4]

**Where the standard requires a valve, it isn't optional**, whichever option the customer chose. If the scope is missing something the standard requires, call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) before you start. [VERIFY: which valves AS/NZS 3500.4 requires on a replacement unit]

Follow the manufacturer's installation instructions as well. An install that ignores them can put the manufacturer's warranty at risk.

> **On the job:** On a like-for-like swap in Winston Hills, you find the old PTR drain line has been cut short and dumps into a garden bed. You photograph it and raise it with the Install Manager before you start, because the new install has to be compliant and the scope needs to cover it.

## Gas connection and flue requirements

**Only do gas work your licence covers.** If you don't hold a gas licence, you don't touch the gas.

Before you connect:

- **Check the gas type.** Natural gas and LPG units aren't interchangeable. Match the unit to the gas on site.
- **Check the supply suits the unit.** A continuous flow unit can need far more gas than the old storage unit did. If the pipe or meter looks undersized, call the Install Manager before you start.
- **Check the flue position.** Flues need clearance from windows, doors, other openings and boundaries. [VERIFY: flue clearance rules in the current gas installation standard] A spot that suited the old unit may not suit the new one.

Then connect, test and commission:

1. Make the connection and leak test it before anything is lit.
2. Commission the unit to the manufacturer's instructions.
3. Check the flue is clear and working, and the burner runs properly.
4. Photograph the connection, the flue and the unit's data plate.

Then lodge the compliance paperwork on time, as covered in Safety & Compliance on Installs.

> **On the job:** In a Telopea townhouse, the new continuous flow unit is going on the same wall as the old one. Since the old unit went in, the neighbour has added a window close by. Check the flue clearance before you mount anything. If it won't comply, call the Install Manager before the unit is on the wall, not after.

## Delivery temperature and tempering [VERIFY AS/NZS 3500.4]

Hot water has two jobs that pull in different directions. It has to be **stored hot enough** to control bacteria such as Legionella, and **delivered cool enough** to the bathroom that nobody gets scalded.

The tempering valve makes both possible. The tank stays hot, and the water at the shower, bath and basin arrives at a safe temperature.

The exact limits, and which outlets they apply to, are set by AS/NZS 3500.4 and NSW rules. Check the current figures. Don't go from memory, or from what the last plumber did. [VERIFY AS/NZS 3500.4: current storage and delivery temperature limits, and which outlets they apply to]

On every install:

- Fit the tempering valve where the standard requires it, and set it to the required limit.
- Test the delivery temperature at the bathroom after commissioning, and record the reading on the job with a photo.
- Explain to the customer why the bathroom hot water is set where it is.

Scalds are most dangerous for young children and older people. Never set it hotter because a customer asks.

> **Say it like this:** "I can't set the bathroom water hotter than the standard allows. It's there to stop scalds, especially for kids and older people."

> **On the job:** An owner in Wahroonga asks you to turn the bathroom hot water up "like the old one". You show her the tempering valve and the reading you recorded, explain what it's protecting against, and leave it where the standard says.

## Recording the unit: serial, install date, warranty registration

The job isn't finished when the water's hot. **Record the new unit in ServiceTitan equipment records**, every time:

- the make and model
- the **serial number**
- the **install date**
- the **warranty registration**

Photograph the unit's data plate too, so the serial can be checked later without a trip back.

**Register the manufacturer warranty on the day.** Left for later, it gets forgotten, and the customer finds out when they need it. [CONFIRM: who registers manufacturer warranties, the installer or the office]

Why it matters:

- warranty claims are quicker when the serial and install date are on file
- the next tech can see what's installed before they knock
- the unit's age drives future follow-up, because units around 8 years or older get flagged

Your sign-off for this module is a **supervised hot water install, signed off by a lead installer: one electric, and one gas or continuous flow**.

> **On the job:** Years from now, a customer in Kellyville calls with a fault on the unit you installed. Because you recorded the serial, install date and warranty registration, the office can check the warranty straight away instead of sending someone out to read the plate.
