## Building Essential / Standard / Premium / Elite on your tablet from templates

Once you've diagnosed the problem and run the health check, build the options in ServiceTitan on your tablet, at the job, before you say a single price.

**Every job with more than one sensible fix gets all four options**: Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite. Once the health check is done, that's most jobs.

- **Start from a template.** The five standard bundles (Hot Water, Whole-Home Water Protection, Bathroom Refresh, Drain Fix-for-Good and Landlord Compliance Pack) and your other templates give you the lines, wording and hours in a few taps.
- **Bundle what you found.** Essential fixes what they called about. Standard, Premium and Elite add the related items in your photos. Never quote just the one item.
- **Take out anything you didn't find.** A template is a starting point, not a script.
- **Every line comes from the pricebook.** No hand-typed prices, no bundle discounts and no improvising. If you can't find a price, don't make one up. Call the office.

If you keep building the same combination by hand, it should probably be a template. Suggest it to Harriet (Sales Manager).

> **On the job:** You're at a Dundas home for a running toilet. The health check finds a dripping shower mixer and perished flexi hoses. Essential fixes the cistern. Standard adds new cistern internals and flexis. Premium adds a new shower mixer. Elite adds isolation valves and a Care Plan. Every line is backed by a photo, and the customer chooses.

## Presenting at the kitchen table

Sit down where the customer can see the tablet, and follow the same order every time: **the problem, the fix, the options, the Proximity promise, then let them choose.**

- **Show the photos first.** Showing beats telling.
- **Never prejudge.** Present all four options at every door. Their choice reveals the budget, not your guess.
- **Lead with Elite** with confidence, then narrow to the two that fit this home.
- **Talk about what it means for them.** People buy peace of mind, not parts, so listen more than you talk. (A principle from US service trainer Crisara of Service MVP, in our own words.)
- **Scope. Price. Silence.** Say the price, then stop and let the customer speak first. Never discount to fill the quiet.
- **On bigger jobs, offer payment options** before they ask. [CONFIRM: HomePro for Service Techs?] Finance sits outside ServiceTitan and never appears on the proposal.

If a customer says upfront they only want the one thing fixed, **respect it.** Give them the price, fix what they asked for, then briefly show what you found. If they want to know more, present the options, with no pressure.

> **On the job:** In Westmead, a customer says, "Just fix the leak. I don't want to be sold anything." You fix the leak, then say, "All done. While I was under there I took a couple of photos you might want to see." She asks what sorting it out would cost. Now you present the options, Elite first.

## Doing it today vs booking it as a planned job (50% deposit)

Once the customer chooses, the job goes one of two ways.

**If they choose work that can be done today, you finish it today.** If you have the time, the parts and the licence for it, don't book it for next week. Do it, test it, and collect payment and sign-off in ServiceTitan before you leave. Work done on the spot is paid on completion. Then offer the Care Plan, as you do on every completed job.

**Bigger work is booked as a planned job, with a 50% deposit and a proper handover:**

1. Get the plan signed in ServiceTitan.
2. Collect the 50% deposit. No deposit means no scheduling and no materials ordered.
3. Complete the handover so the job can be booked with Kim (residential dispatch).
4. Tell the customer what happens next, and that the balance is due on completion.

Don't push work into "planned" because it's easier on the day, and don't squeeze a big job into today if you can't do it properly.

## Member pricing

Care Plan members get member pricing. [CONFIRM: benefits, price, member pricing] Get it right at the kitchen table, every time.

- **Check before you build the options.** Look at the customer's record. If they're a member, they should see member pricing. A member who's shown the standard price feels forgotten.
- **Member pricing comes from the pricebook**, like every other price. It's a benefit of the plan, not a discount you give. You never work it out yourself, and you never give it to someone who isn't a member.
- **For non-members, the plan is part of the options.** The Care Plan already sits in the Elite option of the standard bundles. [CONFIRM: whether a customer who joins today gets member pricing on today's job]
- **Offer the Care Plan on every completed job**, whatever they chose. Module s8 covers how.

> **Say it like this:** "You're on our Care Plan, so these are your member prices. Thanks for being with us."

> **On the job:** A customer in Pennant Hills mentions she joined the Care Plan last year. You'd already checked her record before building the options, so the prices on your tablet are her member prices. She notices, and it's one less thing for her to question.

## If you sold a planned job, you're its job captain: the handover, the installer's questions, and the finish, with the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) owning payment and the customer's happiness

Sales Techs sell and hand over, and that's the end of their part. **Service Techs are different: if you sold a planned job, you captain it.**

As captain, you own three things:

- **The handover.** Scope (what's in and what's not), materials and specials, photos and video, access, estimated hours, and the customer's notes and expectations. The test: could the installer do this job without ever calling you?
- **The installer's questions.** You saw the site. When the installer has a question about what you sold, answer it quickly. If they find something different on the day, they stop and call the Install Manager, and you help get the scope right.
- **The finish.** The job should finish the way you sold it. [CONFIRM: what the job captain does at the finish]

What you don't own: **the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) owns collecting the balance and the customer's happiness.** If the customer raises a concern about the install or the payment, tell Charlotte straight away so she can look after it.

Your hours matter too. They set the installer's pay and the job's profit. Under-scope it, and the scope-change calls and warranty returns come back to you.

> **On the job:** The day before a re-pipe you sold in Toongabbie, the installer rings: where does the new line run under the house? Your subfloor photos and notes have the answer. It's a two-minute call instead of a lost morning.
