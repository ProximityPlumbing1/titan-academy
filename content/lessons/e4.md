## Present in person where possible, with the photos to hand

A proposal works best face to face. **Present in person wherever you can, with the photos and video on the screen in front of you.**

In person, you can see how the customer reacts, answer a question before it turns into a worry, and point to the exact thing you found. An emailed quote is just a number. With nothing behind it, the customer compares it with the cheapest price they can find.

Before you sit down:

- Have your photos and video open, in the order you'll talk about them.
- Sit beside the customer, not across the table, so you're both looking at the same screen.
- Check the people making the decision are there. If a partner or a landlord decides, ask if they can join by phone, or agree a time to walk them through it.

**Showing beats telling.** Customers can't see inside the wall, the drain or the tank. Their own photos let them see what you saw and make up their own minds.

> **On the job:** You've just inspected a leaking hot water unit in Carlingford. The owner is on her laptop and says, "Just email me the quote, I'm flat out." An emailed price is a number with nothing behind it. How do you get five minutes at the kitchen table first?

> **Say it like this:** "It'll take five minutes. I'll show you exactly what I found before you see a single price."

## Show the problem, then the fix, then each option in plain language

Every presentation follows the same order:

1. **The problem.** What you found, shown in their photos and video.
2. **The fix.** What it takes to solve it properly.
3. **The options.** Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite, with Elite first.
4. **The promise.** What they get with Proximity (the next lesson).
5. **Their choice.** You stop and let them decide.

Keep it in plain English. Lead with what the problem means for them, not the name of the part.

**Never prejudge what someone can afford.** A modest house is not a modest budget, and you can't see wealth or debt from the driveway. Present every option at every door, with the same confidence. The customer decides what they can afford, not us.

Don't read the options like a menu. Lead with Elite, then narrow to the two that fit this home.

> **Say it like this:** "The option I'd put in my own home is Elite. Let me show you why, and then what sits below it."

> **On the job:** An older weatherboard in Blacktown, and the owner mentions money's been tight. The hot water unit is rusting at the base. You show the photo, explain it needs replacing, then present all four options, starting with Elite: an upgraded unit, new valves and flexis, and a Care Plan. She chooses Standard. Her choice, not your guess.

## What they get with us: fixed upfront price, lifetime labour warranty, same-day return, no hidden costs

After the options, tell the customer what they get with us. This is what they're really paying for: certainty.

- **A fixed upfront price.** They know the cost before we start. We never charge by the hour, so if the job takes longer, that's on us, not them.
- **A lifetime labour warranty.** We stand behind our work. [CONFIRM: approved wording, including what's excluded]
- **Same-day return.** If something isn't right, we come back the same day. [CONFIRM: exactly what this covers]
- **No hidden costs.** If something changes once we start, we stop, show them and get their OK before doing anything extra.

The promise is your answer when a customer says another quote was cheaper. We don't compete on price. We compete on trust and quality, and the promise is what that looks like on the day.

Explain it the same way every time, using the approved wording. Never invent your own version, like "lifetime warranty on everything", and never offer to beat another quote.

> **Say it like this:** "With us you get a fixed upfront price, no hidden costs, a lifetime labour warranty and same-day return. If anything changes once we start, I'll show you and get your OK first."

> **On the job:** A customer in Kings Langley says the other plumber was a few hundred dollars cheaper. You don't knock the other plumber and you don't drop the price. You ask what their quote included, then walk through what comes with ours.

## No pressure: if they're not ready, agree a follow-up time and log it

**Scope. Price. Silence.** Once you've walked through the scope and said the price, stop talking. The customer speaks first.

The silence feels long to you, not to them. Count to five in your head if you need to. Most techs discount before the customer has even objected. Don't justify, don't explain it all again, and never discount to fill the quiet. You can't change a price anyway: only Harriet (Sales Manager) or Sadie (Office Manager) can.

If they hesitate, stay calm and curious. Find out whether the worry is the price or the value, then clarify the options and the promise. If they want it cheaper, a smaller option has fewer lines. It's never the same work for less.

If they're not ready, that's fine. "Not yet" is a normal answer.

1. Agree a specific time to follow up.
2. Agree what they'll decide by then.
3. Log it in ServiceTitan before you leave.
4. Follow up when you said you would. Harriet also follows up quotes, but the one you agreed is yours.

No fake urgency, and no "sign today and I'll do you a deal".

> **Say it like this:** "Totally fair. Is it the overall cost that worries you, or whether it's worth it?"

> **Say it like this:** "No worries. When's a good time for me to follow up, Thursday or Friday?"
