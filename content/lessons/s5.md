## Electric: elements, thermostats, cut-outs

**No hot water is often urgent, especially with vulnerable occupants like elderly people, babies or anyone unwell. Restore it today if at all possible.** Hot water calls peak from June to August, so this module is refreshed every April, before the winter peak.

An electric storage unit has three main parts to check:

- **The element** heats the water. A failed one means no hot water, or a safety switch that keeps tripping.
- **The thermostat** switches the element on and off. A faulty one can mean no heat, lukewarm water or overheating.
- **The over-temperature cut-out** shuts the element off if the water gets too hot. Find out why it tripped before you reset it. One that keeps tripping often points to the thermostat.

Check the simple things first: the switchboard, the safety switch, and whether the unit is on an off-peak tariff that only heats at certain times. Sometimes the household has just used up the tank.

**Isolate the power and test that it's dead before you touch anything.** Only do the electrical work your licence covers. [VERIFY: the NSW licence needed to test or replace elements and thermostats] [CONFIRM: which service techs hold that licence]

## Gas storage: pilot, thermocouple, gas control

A gas storage unit uses a small pilot flame to light the main burner. Three parts cause most faults:

- **The pilot.** If it's out, there's no hot water. A draught can blow it out, and a dirty pilot may not stay lit.
- **The thermocouple** senses the pilot flame and holds the gas valve open. If it fails, the gas control shuts the gas off, even with a good pilot. That's a safety feature. **Never bypass it.**
- **The gas control** combines the thermostat and the safety shut-off. If the pilot and thermocouple are fine but the burner won't come on, look here.

Check the basics first: is the gas on at the meter, or is the LPG bottle empty? Is the flue clear and intact?

Scorch marks around the burner door, soot, a yellow lazy flame or a gas smell can mean the unit isn't burning or venting properly. Turn it off, don't relight it, and flag it.

**Gas work is licensed work.** Only do what your licence covers, and leak test before you leave. [CONFIRM: which techs hold gas licences]

> **On the job:** In Strathfield, the pilot on a gas unit lights but goes out a few seconds after you let go of the button. The flame looks clean and strong. That points to the thermocouple, not the gas supply.

## Continuous flow error codes

Continuous flow units heat water as it flows, so there's no tank to run out. When something goes wrong, most show an **error code**.

**Codes differ between brands and models.** Don't diagnose from memory. Look the code up for that exact model in the manufacturer's manual first.

Common causes behind the codes:

- **No ignition or flame failure**: the gas is off, the LPG bottle is empty, or the gas supply is low.
- **No power**: gas units still need power to ignite.
- **Low flow**: a blocked inlet filter, or a water-saving tap or shower rose that doesn't draw enough water to keep the unit firing.
- **Overheating or sensor faults**: check the manual for what's safe to test on site.

Before you pull a unit apart, **check whether it's still under the manufacturer's warranty**. [CONFIRM: how we handle faults on units still under the manufacturer's warranty]

Record the make, model, serial number and error code, with photos of the display and data plate. You'll need them if parts or a warranty claim follow.

> **On the job:** A customer in Rouse Hill says the hot water "comes and goes" in the shower. The unit shows no error, but they've just fitted a water-saving shower rose. Check the flow before you blame the unit.

## Heat pumps

A heat pump works like a fridge in reverse. It pulls heat from the outside air into the water, so it uses less electricity than a standard electric unit. Many have a backup element for cold weather or heavy use.

Common problems:

- **Poor airflow.** A unit boxed in, or with leaves and garden waste against it, works harder and heats more slowly.
- **Slow recovery on cold mornings.** Some slowdown can be normal. Check the settings and the backup element before you call it a fault.
- **Fan or compressor not running.** Check the power, the settings and any error code.
- **A blocked condensate drain.** Heat pumps make condensation, and the drain has to run freely.
- **Noise**: rattles, humming or vibration.

**Don't open the refrigerant circuit.** Refrigerant work needs its own licence. [VERIFY: refrigerant handling licence requirements for heat pump water heaters]

In the Hot Water bundle, Elite can be an upgraded unit: a heat pump or continuous flow where suitable. Suitable means room, good airflow and a spot where the noise won't bother bedrooms or neighbours.

> **On the job:** A customer in Glenwood says their heat pump "isn't keeping up". It's squeezed behind the side fence with garden waste piled against the fan. Clearing the airflow may be the whole fix. Show them, and explain how to keep it clear.

## Relief and tempering valves

**The relief valve** (pressure and temperature relief valve) on a storage unit lets water out if the pressure or temperature gets too high. It's a safety device. **Never plug, cap or disable one.**

A little water from the relief drain while the unit heats can be normal. A constant flow usually isn't. Look for:

- **high mains pressure**, often fixed with a pressure-limiting valve
- **a worn relief valve** that no longer seals
- **overheating** from a faulty thermostat

**The tempering valve** mixes hot and cold so the water reaching the bathroom is at a safe temperature, reducing the risk of scalds, while the unit stores water hotter to control bacteria. A worn one can deliver water that's too hot, too cool, or swinging between the two. [VERIFY: current NSW storage and delivery temperature limits, and when a tempering valve must be fitted]

Many older homes don't have a tempering valve. That's a finding to record and explain calmly.

In the Hot Water bundle, the tempering valve is in Standard, and the relief drain check and pressure-limiting valve are in Premium. > **Say it like this:** "This valve is a safety device. It's letting water out because your mains pressure is high. A new valve on its own won't fix that, but a pressure-limiting valve will."

## Repair or replace across the four options on the spot (the 8+ year flag)

Hot water units around 8 years or older get flagged. Tag them for follow-up (for example, hot-water-8yr+).

When an older unit fails, **present repair vs replace across the four options, Elite first, and let the customer choose.** For an ageing electric unit with a failed element:

- **Elite**: an upgraded unit (heat pump or continuous flow where suitable), everything in Premium, and a Care Plan.
- **Premium**: everything in Standard, plus flexi hoses at the unit, a relief drain check and a pressure-limiting valve.
- **Standard**: a like-for-like unit with a tempering valve and a new isolation valve.
- **Essential**: replace the element and get hot water back today.

## Gas appliance checks [CONFIRM licensed staff]

On some jobs you'll check gas appliances: cooktops, ovens, heaters and gas hot water. **Gas work is licensed work. If you don't hold a gas licence, your job is to notice, not to fix.** [CONFIRM: which techs hold gas licences]

What to look for:

- **The flame.** Crisp and blue is normal. Yellow, lazy or lifting flames can mean it isn't burning properly.
- **Soot or scorch marks** around the appliance or the flue.
- **Ventilation and flues**: vents blocked or covered, or a damaged flue.
- **A gas smell** at the appliance, the meter or along the pipe.
- **Age, condition and service history.**

Poor burning can produce carbon monoxide, which you can't see or smell. If an appliance looks unsafe, turn it off, tell the customer clearly not to use it, photograph it and flag it 'Needs attention now' so the office calls the same day. [VERIFY: current NSW procedure for an unsafe gas appliance, including disconnection and notices]

If you smell gas, follow the gas safety steps from Field Foundations. Explain it calmly: it's a safety finding, not a sales line.

> **Say it like this:** "I've turned this heater off because it isn't burning cleanly. I'd recommend you don't use it until a licensed gasfitter has serviced it. I've taken photos, and the office will follow it up with you today."
