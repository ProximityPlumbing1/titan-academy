## The Proximity workmanship standard [CONFIRM: written standard?]

We promise a lifetime labour warranty. That only works if the work is good enough to stand behind for life. The workmanship standard is what every install has to meet before you call it finished. [CONFIRM: written workmanship standard per job type]

Until there's a written standard for each job type, these are the basics on every install:

- **To the scope**: nothing missing, and nothing extra without signed approval (i8).
- **To the manufacturer's instructions** and the standards that apply to the work.
- **Neat**: pipework straight and supported, fixtures square and secure, no smeared sealant or loose ends.
- **Tested**: pressure, flow and leaks, every time (next lesson).
- **Clean**: worked cleanly throughout, and the site left cleaner than you found it.
- **Documented**: before-and-after photos and video saved to the job.

The simple test: would you be happy for the next plumber to open the cupboard and see your name on it?

> **On the job:** Two installers fit the same hot water unit in Penshurst. One leaves the pipework straight, supported and tidy. The other leaves it working but crooked, with sealant smeared down the wall. Both pass a leak test. Does the second one meet the standard? What would the customer think if they photographed it for a review?

## Testing before leaving: pressure, flow and leak checks

**Nothing leaves site untested: pressure, flow and leaks.** "It looks fine" is not a test.

- **Pressure.** Pressure test new pipework before it's covered, and again at the end where the job calls for it. [VERIFY: test pressure and duration for each job type] Check the mains pressure too: if it's high, that's a genuine finding for the customer (i9).
- **Flow.** Run every outlet you've touched, hot and cold, at full flow. Listen for air and water hammer.
- **Leaks.** Check every joint you've made, then look again after the water has been running for a while. Check under the sink, behind the pan and at the unit. A meter test shows a leak you can't see.
- **Drains.** Run water through anything you've connected and check that it clears and doesn't leak.

Test before the walk-through, not during it. You want to be the one who finds the drip, not the customer.

> **On the job:** You finish a vanity in Dee Why and everything looks dry. A few minutes after running the tap, there's a bead of water on the waste nut. Found now, it's a quick fix. Found by the customer next week, it's a return trip and a dent in trust.

> **Say it like this:** "Before I pack up, I test everything I've touched: pressure, flow and leaks. That way you're not the one who finds a problem."

## Commissioning checklist

Commissioning means proving that what you installed works the way it should, then setting it up for the customer. Each job type has its own checks. [CONFIRM: the commissioning checklist for each job type, and whether it's a ServiceTitan form]

The kind of things it covers:

1. Water and gas back on, and every isolation valve you touched open and working.
2. Pressure, flow and leak checks done.
3. **Hot water**: the unit filled and purged of air before the power or gas goes on, the relief valve checked, and the delivered temperature checked at the outlets. [VERIFY AS/NZS 3500.4]
4. **Gas**: connections tested and the appliance running correctly. [VERIFY: gas testing and compliance requirements]
5. **Fixtures**: hot and cold the right way round, toilets filling and stopping, wastes clearing.
6. **Records**: a new unit added to ServiceTitan equipment records, and the manufacturer warranty registered on the day. [CONFIRM who does this]
7. **Paperwork**: compliance certificates lodged on time (i4). [VERIFY]

For this module's sign-off, **you complete the testing and commissioning checklist on a live job**, and a lead installer checks it.

> **On the job:** On a hot water install in Oran Park, the checklist says to check the delivered temperature at the outlets. The bathroom basin is running hotter than it should. You adjust the tempering valve and test again, before anyone in the house turns that tap on.

## Photographing the finished work

**The finished-work photos are your proof.** They show what you did, how well you did it, and that you left the site clean.

Take them before the tools go back in the van:

- the finished work, close up and wide, so anyone can tell where it is
- the details: valves, connections, supports and serial plates
- anything that's about to be hidden, like pipe in a wall or floor before it's covered
- the clean work area
- the same angles as your before photos, so the two sit side by side

Save them to the job in ServiceTitan, numbered and referred to in your notes (f5).

They're your proof three ways:

- **For the warranty.** If something goes wrong later, the photos show what was done and how. Under a lifetime labour warranty, that record matters for years.
- **For your sign-offs.** Charlotte (interim Install Manager) signs off job-related install items from your job photos and video.
- **For the customer.** They're the "after" in the walk-through (i10).

> **On the job:** A customer in Mosman calls about a damp patch near the laundry wall you worked on months ago. Your photos show the joints, the test and the dry cupboard on the day. The office can see straight away whether it's our work or something new.

## Why the lifetime labour warranty and same-day return mean getting it right first time

Because we stand behind the labour for life, **getting it right first time matters even more.**

- **The customer never pays for a return.** Every return under the lifetime labour warranty is **unpaid time and a dent in trust.**
- **A return wipes out the time you saved** and costs the business its profit on the job (i1).
- **The same-day return** means that if something isn't right, we come back the same day. [CONFIRM: exactly what this covers] That's a van pulled off paid work, and another customer's job moved.
- **Returns are tracked.** Warranty and same-day returns are one of the numbers installers are measured on, and a lot of them signals a quality problem.

Speed without quality is failure. Saving an hour today and coming back for two next month is a loss for you, the business and the customer.

The fix is everything in this module: work to the standard, test before you leave, commission properly and photograph the result.

> **Say it like this:** "Our labour is covered for life, so I'd rather spend a few more minutes checking now than see you again for the wrong reason."
