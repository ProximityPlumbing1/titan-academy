## What counts as an emergency

An emergency is anything causing damage or risk right now, or about to. The usual ones:

- **An active leak**, especially water coming through a ceiling or a wall
- **A burst pipe**, or water that can't be turned off
- **Flooding**, inside or under the house
- **Sewage overflowing** inside or around the home
- **No hot water**, which is often urgent, especially with babies, elderly or unwell people in the home
- **A suspected gas leak.** Follow your gas safety training and only do gas work your licence covers. [VERIFY: our gas emergency procedure and who to call]

Not every after-hours call is an emergency. A dripping tap, a slow-filling cistern or a squeaky tap handle can wait until tomorrow. Treat those customers with the same courtesy anyway.

**Emergency customers remember how they were treated for life.** On their worst night, you're the person who turns up. Stop the damage, stay calm, look after them, and a crisis becomes a customer for life.

> **On the job:** It's 9pm in Strathfield. Nothing's leaking, but the hot water has been out since the morning, and the customer's elderly mother lives with her. Is this an emergency? Think about who's in the house, not just what's broken.

## Make safe first: isolate, contain, protect

At an emergency, **make it safe before anything else**: before the quote, before the photos and before the conversation about rates.

1. **Isolate.** Turn off the water at the nearest isolation valve, or at the main stop tap, which is usually at the water meter. If water has reached lights, power points or the switchboard, keep everyone clear and don't touch anything electrical. [VERIFY: our procedure when water reaches electrics, including who isolates the power]
2. **Contain.** Stop the water spreading with buckets, towels and drop sheets. Never stand under a sagging ceiling.
3. **Protect.** Move what you can out of the way, and cover floors and belongings.

Once it's under control, take photos and video of the damage and of what you did. An emergency can become an insurance claim, so record precise facts: where the water came from, what failed and what you isolated.

Sewage jobs need the right PPE and hygiene, every time. And as on any job, **if it's unsafe, stop and ask.** Anyone can stop a job that isn't safe, and nobody is ever criticised for it.

> **On the job:** Water is pouring through a kitchen ceiling in Kogarah at 9pm, and the customer wants to know what it'll cost. You say, "Let me stop the water first," and head straight for the stop tap. A minute later the flow has slowed to a drip. Now you can talk.

What doesn't change after hours:

- **A fixed upfront price.** We never charge by the hour, at 2pm or at 2am. The customer knows the price before you do the work. [CONFIRM: how the make-safe is priced after hours]
- **No hidden costs.** If something changes, stop, show them and get their approval first.
- **No discounts.** Only Harriet or Sadie can change a price. "I'll do it cheap" is a discount, whatever time of night you say it.

Turning off the water comes first. Then explain the price calmly:

- **Acknowledge the stress before the numbers.** An angry question about price is usually fear talking.
- **Explain it plainly**, in a few sentences, then stop talking and let them decide.
- **Never be dismissive.** "Not my problem" loses the customer for life. So does ignoring the question.

> **Say it like this:** "I know this is stressful. The water's off now, so nothing else is getting damaged. Here's how the after-hours price works, and you'll know the full price before I do anything else."

## Stressed customers

A customer with water through the ceiling isn't thinking straight. They may be panicking, rude or angry about the rate. **Don't take it personally. They're scared, not your enemy.**

- **Act first.** Nothing calms a panicking customer faster than the water stopping.
- **Acknowledge it.** "I know this is stressful" goes a long way.
- **Tell them what's happening and what comes next**, in short, plain sentences. Not knowing is the scary part.
- **Give them something to do** if it helps: finding towels, or keeping the kids and pets clear.
- **Stay calm and slow.** Your tone sets theirs.
- **Never argue, blame or promise a refund or discount.** If it turns into a complaint, stay calm, call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) or the office, and log it. [CONFIRM: who takes on-site complaints after hours]

Emergency customers remember how fast you stopped the water, how calm you were, and whether they felt looked after or sold to.

> **On the job:** At 9pm in Lidcombe, the customer is shouting before you're out of the van. You don't argue. You say, "Show me where it's coming from, and let's get the water off." With the water off and drop sheets down, he apologises for yelling. Now he's ready to listen.

## Handing over the permanent fix

Once it's safe, the job isn't finished. **Make safe first, then present the four options for the permanent fix.**

Tonight the customer has lived the worst version of the problem. You don't need to describe what happens if they do nothing. Just explain what failed, why, and what fixing it properly looks like.

- **Find the root cause.** A burst in old pipework is rarely the only weak point. It's usually an ageing system failing in stages.
- **Build all four options from what you found**, Elite first, every line from the pricebook.
- **Let them choose, with no pressure.** If they're too rattled to decide tonight, agree when you'll follow up and log it in ServiceTitan.
- **Work you can do now, safely and properly, gets done now.** Bigger work is booked as a planned job with a 50% deposit and a proper handover.
- **Write your notes for whoever picks it up next**: Why called, What I found, What I did, What I recommend, with numbered photos.
- **Offer the Care Plan** once the job is complete.

> **On the job:** A burst copper pipe under a house in Turramurra. You've made it safe, but the rest of the pipework is the same age and just as corroded. Essential repairs the burst section. Elite re-pipes the house and adds a Care Plan, with Standard and Premium in between. She wants to sleep on it, so you agree a time to call tomorrow and log it.
