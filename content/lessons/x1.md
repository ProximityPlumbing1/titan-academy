## Kill complacency: where experienced sellers leave money on the table (autopilot, same pitch, guessing the budget)

Nobody in this group needs the basics. So this program isn't about learning to sell. It's about **the last ten per cent**.

At your level, the risk isn't skill. It's habit. The enemy is **autopilot**:

- **The same pitch**, whatever the house and whoever opens the door.
- **The same options**, because they usually get a yes.
- **The same guess at what the customer can spend**, made before they've seen a number.

None of it feels like losing, because you still close. But every option you edit out in your head, and every question you've stopped asking since year one, is money left on the table.

How much money? One premium US contractor lifted its average ticket from **$23K to $37K, up 62%**, by changing how it sold, not what it sold.

> **On the job:** It's your third hot water lead this week in the same Kellyville estate. Before you've opened the cupboard, you've already picked the unit, the option you'll lead with and what they'll spend. They sign Standard in 40 minutes. Was that a win? What did you skip?

*Source: Momnt case study, Braga Brothers (US), cited in Proximity's October 2026 "Selling the Big Job" session. It's a vendor case study: it shows what's possible, not a guaranteed result.*

## The rules now: the promise (fixed price, lifetime labour warranty, same-day return), four options Elite first, every line from the pricebook, 50% deposit, no free discounts. Only Harriet or Sadie can change a price

You know how to sell. Make sure you're selling to **today's rules**:

- **The promise**, said the same way by everyone: a fixed upfront price, no hidden costs, a lifetime labour warranty and a same-day return. [CONFIRM: approved wording for the lifetime labour warranty and same-day return, including what's excluded]
- **Four options on every job:** Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite, with **Elite presented first**.
- **Every line from the pricebook.** No hand-typed prices and no bundle discounts.
- **Never by the hour**, but every price is built on hours.
- **The 50% deposit**, collected by you at signing. The balance is due on completion.
- **You sell and hand over.** After a complete handover, the job belongs to the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim).
- **No free discounts.** The price only comes down when scope comes out. **Only Harriet (Sales Manager) or Sadie (Office Manager) can change a price.**

Why so strict on discounts? Profit is about 20%: **about $200 on a $1,000 job**. A 10% discount gives away $100, half the profit, for the same work.

Know all this cold? You can test out of the rules lessons with 90% or better.

> **Say it like this:** "I can't change the price, but I can show you the options that fit what you need."

## Your two numbers: average ticket × close rate. Ten jobs at $2,000 vs eleven at $2,200 is +21%, because they multiply

Everything you're measured on comes down to two numbers: **average ticket** and **close rate**. They're the only two Harriet watches as Sales Manager, and the two Dispatch Pro ranks you on.

What most people miss is that they **multiply**:

- **Before:** 10 jobs × $2,000 = $20,000.
- **After:** 11 jobs × $2,200 = $24,200.

That's **+21%** from a 10% bigger ticket and one extra yes. Not 10% plus 10%. They multiply.

They're also your pay. Commission is **5% of sales paid in full**, so a lift in either number shows up in your pocket once the job is paid.

The habits behind both numbers are the ones we'll track every week:

- four options on every job, Elite first
- payment options on every quote, in weekly numbers, before they ask (said out loud, never on the proposal)
- unsold quotes followed up within 48 hours [CONFIRM: the follow-up window for unsold quotes]
- no free discounts

> **On the job:** Think back over last week's leads. One more yes, and a ticket 10% bigger because a customer chose up, is all the +21% takes. Neither needs a single extra lead.

## The $200K floor: 5 leads a day × 60% × $3,387 × 21 days = $213K. Run every lead, every full day

**$200K a month is the floor for every sales truck.** Not the target. The minimum.

Our own best numbers, in one truck: **5 leads a day × 60% close rate × $3,387 average ticket × 21 days = $213K a month.** That's the best close rate and best average ticket on our team this year, at the five leads you already get on a full day. It's past the floor before a single Saturday.

So **you already have the capacity for $200K.** At those numbers, three sold jobs a day is all it takes. The gap isn't more leads or harder selling. It's **full days**:

- Run 3 leads a day: about $128K.
- Run 4: about $171K.
- Run all 5: about $213K.

Then small lifts multiply. **+$250 on the average ticket and +5 points of close rate takes $213K to about $248K a month**, with no extra days.

At 5% commission, the floor is about **$10K a month**. Our best month this year, $277K, earned **$13.9K**. That's commission alone, before wages, Saturdays and overtime.

> **On the job:** Your third lead ends at 1:30pm with a signed Premium job, and the fourth is across the river in Gladesville. Heading home feels earned. But leads four and five are the difference between $128K and $213K.

## Dispatch Pro: it ranks you on close rate × average ticket vs the median for each job type (last 30 days and 12 months). Regular shifts only, skills on file, drive-time limits, recalls excluded

ServiceTitan's **Dispatch Pro** now decides who gets each job: no favourites, no guesswork, just the data. [CONFIRM: whether Dispatch Pro is now live for all sales trucks]

It learns from your sales, average tickets, memberships sold and the leads you generate, then picks the tech most likely to get the best result on each job.

**How it scores you:** your **close rate for that job type × your average ticket against the median for that job type**. The same two numbers, multiplied. It remembers your **last 30 days and last 12 months**.

The rules it plays by:

- **Regular shifts only.** On-call shifts are ignored, so **Saturdays only count if they're rostered as regular shifts**.
- **Skills on file.** You need every skill the job type lists. Check yours with dispatch.
- **Drive time is a hard limit.** It never sends a tech past the maximum.
- **Recalls are excluded.** Recall and warranty jobs are left out. Every other job feeds your score.

Better numbers get you the jobs with the most potential, which lifts your numbers again next month.

> **On the job:** Your skills list was set up years ago and is missing a job type you now quote every week. Until it's on file, Dispatch Pro can't send you those leads. Check your skills with dispatch this week.

## Your commitment card: today's average ticket and close rate, and the numbers you'll hit by week 8

Everyone fills in a commitment card, live, in this session. That includes anyone who tested out of the rules lessons.

It has three lines:

- **My average ticket:** today's number, and the number I'll hit by week 8.
- **My close rate:** today's number, and the number I'll hit by week 8.
- **One habit I start tomorrow.**

"Today's number" is your baseline: your average ticket and close rate over the last 90 days in ServiceTitan. Harriet records it with your week 8 targets, and in week 8 your numbers are checked against it.

Pick targets that stretch you but that you'd put your name to. At our best numbers, +$250 on the ticket and +5 points of close rate together are worth about **$34.8K in sales and $1,742 in commission a month**.

Make the habit specific. "Present better" isn't a habit. "Elite first at every door, starting with my first lead tomorrow" is.

> **Say it like this:** "My average ticket is [today's number] and I'll have it at [target] by week 8. My close rate is [today's number] and I'll have it at [target]. Starting tomorrow, I'll [one habit]."
