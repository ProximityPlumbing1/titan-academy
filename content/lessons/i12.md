## Call vs message

Install jobs are dispatched by residential dispatch (Kim). Dispatch builds the whole day around what you tell them, so how you tell them matters.

The rule:

- **Call** when it's urgent, complex or sensitive: a job will overrun, you're blocked, there's a safety problem, or a customer is unhappy or won't pay.
- **Message** when it's simple and not urgent: a quick heads-up or a confirmation.
- **Update ServiceTitan** for normal status changes, as they happen, so the board stays live.

**Call immediately if a job will overrun.** The moment you know, not when you're already an hour over. An overrun flagged early is a small change to the day. Flagged late, it's a scramble that moves other customers' jobs.

The mistake goes both ways. An urgent problem buried in a text that nobody reads for twenty minutes is a problem nobody is fixing. A call for something a message would cover ties up dispatch.

Install problems (scope, parts, access, customer) also go to the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) straight away.

> **On the job:** You're on a job in Kogarah booked to finish at midday. At 10:30 you can see it will run at least an hour over, and your next customer in Bexley is expecting you after lunch. Do you text dispatch, call now, or wait and see how it goes? What will Kim need from you?

Plumbing problems don't keep office hours. **Saturday and emergency cover are part of the role.** [CONFIRM roster rules]

What that means for you:

- **Overtime.** Some jobs need finishing, and sometimes the day runs long. Flag it to dispatch as early as you can, so it's planned rather than sprung on you or the customer.
- **Saturdays.** Know your roster ahead of time and plan around it. Turning up for a rostered Saturday is part of being reliable.
- **Emergencies.** Burst pipes, major leaks, no water. The customer is at their most stressed, and they remember who turned up and how they were treated.
- **The standard doesn't drop after hours.** Same protection, same photos and notes, same payment and sign-off.

## Warranty and same-day returns: owning your work

Sometimes a job comes back. When it's your work, **fix it promptly and graciously, and learn from it.**

- **Promptly.** The same-day return is part of our promise. [CONFIRM: exactly what this covers] Treat a return as urgent, not as something to fit in later.
- **Graciously.** Don't argue, make excuses or blame the part, the customer or another tech. The customer called because something isn't right. Thank them for telling us.
- **Never charge for our workmanship.** That's what the lifetime labour warranty means. What's covered, and what isn't, is set by the approved wording. [CONFIRM: approved warranty wording, including exclusions]
- **Not sure it's our work?** Don't argue it on the doorstep. Photograph it, note it, and let the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) decide.
- **Learn from it.** What caused it, and what would have caught it before you left? Write the cause in the job notes.

Returns are linked to the original job and tracked. A lot of them signals a quality problem. Every return is unpaid time and a dent in trust, so the best way to own your work is to make returns rare: test, commission and photograph before you leave.

> **Say it like this:** "Thanks for letting us know. Let me have a look and find out what's happened, and we'll get it sorted for you."
