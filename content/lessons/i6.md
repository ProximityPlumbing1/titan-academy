## Tapware, toilets and fixture swaps: the standard sequence

Fixture swaps look simple, which is exactly why they go wrong. **Standard workflows mean every install is right first time**, whoever does the job.

The standard sequence for a tap, mixer, toilet or basin swap:

1. **Check the new fixture against the old one** before anything comes off: connections, sizes, mounting and the model the customer chose.
2. **Isolate and test** the water before you start (Field Foundations, f2).
3. **Protect the area.** Drop sheets down, the basin or pan covered, nothing that can fall on tiles.
4. **Remove the old fixture** and check what's behind it: the isolation valve, the flexi hoses and the wall or floor connection.
5. **Fit the new fixture** to the manufacturer's instructions, with new flexis or seals where the scope includes them.
6. **Test it**: water on slowly, run it hot and cold, and check every joint for leaks.

**Work cleanly throughout, not just at the end.** Packaging, old fittings and offcuts go straight into a bag, not onto the customer's bench.

For this module's sign-off, a lead installer watches you do a fixture install, plus one other job type from this module.

## Re-pipes: planning, materials and access

A re-pipe is a big job, and it's priced on hours. **A re-pipe needs planning: materials and access, sorted before you start.**

Before the day:

- **Read the scope and the quote photos.** Know which lines are being replaced, in what pipe, and what's staying.
- **Walk the route in your head**: where the new pipe runs, where it goes through walls, floors or the roof space, and where you'll need to open things up.
- **Check the materials** against the scope the day before (i2). A re-pipe that stops for a supply run loses hours fast.
- **Check the access**: subfloor height, roof space, ceilings, cupboards that need emptying, and anything the customer needs to move.
- **Plan the shut-offs** and tell the customer when the water will be off.

Older homes hide things. Watch for old galvanised pipe, pipes running where you didn't expect, and materials that could contain asbestos, like AC pipe, fibro or old vinyl. Suspected asbestos: stop, photograph it, flag it in ServiceTitan and call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim). Never cut, drill or break it.

Anything you find that isn't in the scope is a scope change (i8). Don't absorb it into the job.

> **On the job:** The plan for a re-pipe in Concord runs the new line through the hallway ceiling, which the customer has just had repainted. Talk through the route and the access holes with them before you start, and check what the scope says about making good.

## Water filtration installs

Customers often buy filtration for their family's health or the taste of their water, so they care how easy it is to live with.

- **Confirm what was sold.** An under-sink filter, a filter on a single tap and whole-home filtration on the incoming main are different jobs.
- **Check the site against the scope** before you start: space in the cupboard, where the unit will mount, access to the main, and room to change cartridges later.
- **Isolate first**, install to the manufacturer's instructions, and flush the filter the way the manufacturer says before you hand it over.
- **Leave it serviceable.** Mount it where the cartridge can be changed without emptying the cupboard, and fit isolation valves where the scope includes them.
- **Record it.** Photograph the unit and its label, and note the make and model in the job notes.

Then show the customer how it works and how the cartridge comes out.

> **On the job:** In a Cronulla kitchen, the under-sink filter would fit neatly at the very back of the cupboard, but nobody would ever reach the cartridge. You mount it where it can be serviced and show the customer how to change it. That's the difference between installed and installed well.

> **Say it like this:** "Here's your isolation valve, and this is how the cartridge comes out. The manufacturer's guide tells you when to change it."

## Bathroom rough-in and fit-off (working with other trades)

Bathroom work usually happens in two stages: **rough-in** before the walls and floor are finished, and **fit-off** once they are. In between, other trades do their part. **On renovations, know which trade owns what.**

- **Waterproofing**: check the scope for who owns it. If it isn't us, don't touch it.
- **Tiling**: the tiler sets the finished surface, so your rough-in positions and depths have to suit the tiles and fixtures.
- **Electrical**: only a licensed electrician does electrical work. Never connect or move wiring yourself.

At rough-in:

- set out every outlet from the plans or the fixtures the customer chose
- test your pipework before the walls are closed
- photograph and video everything before it's covered, because once it's tiled those photos are the only record of what's behind the wall

At fit-off:

- protect the new tiles and fixtures, which are some of the most expensive surfaces in the house
- fit, test and clean as you go

If another trade's work stops you, call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim). Don't fix another trade's work yourself.

> **On the job:** At fit-off in a Randwick bathroom, you notice a nick in the new waterproofing beside the shower waste. It's not your trade and not your fault, but if you fit off over it, it becomes your leak. Photograph it, stop, and call the Install Manager.
