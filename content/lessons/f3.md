## Job statuses, forms and timesheets updated in real time, not at the end of the week

**Statuses, forms and timesheets are updated as you go. Never at the end of the week.**

The easiest way to make it automatic is to tie it to your tools: **tools out, timer on. Tools away, timer off.**

- **Statuses** tell dispatch and the office where every job is up to, so they can plan the day and answer the customer who rings to ask.
- **Forms** capture what the job needs recorded. Fill each one in at the point it belongs to, not from memory afterwards.
- **Timesheets** record your actual time. Start when you start, stop when you stop.

Why real time matters:

- **Memory is unreliable.** Times rebuilt on a Friday afternoon are guesses, and guesses are usually wrong.
- **The system can only reward what it can see.** For installers, hours saved against the estimate only count if the time is recorded properly.
- **Honest times fix bad estimates.** Even when a job runs long, accurate time gets the hours right for the next one.

> **On the job:** You finish a re-pipe in Drummoyne, pack up, grab lunch and drive to the next job before you realise the first one is still running in the app. On the record, it took over an hour longer than it really did. Those are hours you actually saved, and now they're gone.

## Reading the job brief, customer history, equipment and past invoices

**Always check the customer's and the equipment's history before you knock.** Two minutes in the driveway can change the whole visit.

What to read:

- **The job brief**: why they called, what was booked, and notes on access, parking, pets or a tenant.
- **Customer history**: past jobs, past recommendations and open estimates. Are they a Care Plan member, a property manager or a strata account?
- **Equipment**: the hot water unit's age and type, and anything we've installed or flagged. Units around 8 years or older get flagged for follow-up.
- **Past invoices**: what we've already done for them, and when.

What it tells you:

- **Is this our work coming back?** It may be a warranty or same-day return. Handle it graciously, and make sure it's linked to the original job.
- **What did we recommend last time?** That's a natural conversation, not a cold pitch.
- **Who decides?** An owner, a property manager or a strata committee.

It also shows the customer you know their home. "I see we looked after your hot water a few years back" beats "So, what's the problem?"

> **On the job:** Before knocking at a Pennant Hills home, you open the history. We replaced the kitchen mixer eight months ago, and today's call is a leak under the same sink. You walk in knowing it might be our work, and you treat it that way: no excuses, just fix it properly.

## Photos, videos and job notes saved to the job

**If it isn't in ServiceTitan, it didn't happen.** That covers your notes, photos, videos, time, payment and sign-off.

Photos on your camera roll, a text to the office or a note on a scrap of paper don't count. Nobody else can find them, they can't go into a customer report, and they won't help you when a job is questioned months later.

The habits:

- **Save photos and video to the job as you go**, not at the end of the day.
- **Before and after, on every job.** The next module covers exactly what to shoot.
- **Write the note while you're still on site**: Why called, What I found, What I did, What I recommend.
- **Number your photos and refer to them in the note.**
- **Record the "all good" items too.** They show you checked.

More people read your notes than you'd think: the office, dispatch, the installer on a planned job, whoever writes the customer report, and the next tech who visits. Write for them.

> **On the job:** A customer in Northmead rings three weeks after a tap job and says you "never touched the hose that's now leaking". Your before-and-after photos in the job show the new hose going in. One look and the call is over. If those photos had stayed on your phone, nobody in the office could have seen them.

## Searching the pricebook and estimate templates

**Every price comes from the pricebook or an approved template. No hand-typed prices.** Not a rounded number, not a "mate's rate", and not what you charged last time.

The pricebook is how the same job gets the same price, whichever tech quotes it. That consistency is what makes a fixed upfront price fair, and the prices are built to protect the profit that keeps the business running.

Getting quick at it:

- **Search by keyword or browse by category**, and check you've picked the right item, not just the first one that comes up.
- **Use approved estimate templates** for common jobs, including the five standard bundles: Hot Water, Whole-Home Water Protection, Bathroom Refresh, Drain Fix-for-Good and Landlord Compliance Pack.
- **Build all four options**, Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite, with Elite presented first. Every line comes from the pricebook, with no bundle discounts.
- **Practise when it's quiet**, so you're not hunting through the app with the customer watching.

## Invoicing, taking payment and customer sign-off

**No job is complete until it's invoiced, paid and signed off in ServiceTitan, before you leave.** The Payment, Completion & Reviews module covers the detail.

Before you build the invoice:

- **Check it matches the work**: the option the customer chose, plus any extra work they approved and signed for, and nothing else.
- **Check what's already been paid.** On planned work, the 50% deposit was paid at approval and the balance is due on completion.

Then, with the customer:

1. **Walk them through the finished work** and the before-and-after photos.
2. **Explain the warranty** in the approved wording.
3. **Take the payment in ServiceTitan**, against the right job.
4. **Get their sign-off in the app.**
5. **Check it all went through**: paid, signed and showing complete.

On installs, the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) owns payment collection, and you collect the balance on site.

A payment that isn't recorded against the job, or a job left open, looks unpaid to the office. A customer who has already paid then gets chased.

Your sign-off for this module is running a full job in the app, start to finish, while supervised: status, notes, photos and video, invoice, payment and sign-off.

> **On the job:** A customer in Bankstown pays for a hot water repair, but you leave before the payment shows against the job. A week later, Chloe rings to chase the "unpaid" invoice. The customer is furious, and fair enough. A few seconds checking the screen would have saved it.

## Tags and raising a lead or follow-up

Not everything you spot gets fixed today. **Tags and leads make sure what you found doesn't get lost.**

**Tags** flag a customer or their equipment for follow-up later, like hot-water-8yr+, ageing-flexi-hose or PRV-monitor. Use the standard list only. A tag you made up drives nothing. [CONFIRM: your standard tag list]

**A lead or follow-up** is for work the customer wants priced, or that needs another visit or another tech. Raise it in ServiceTitan with photos and notes, so whoever picks it up sees exactly what you saw. **Never just "mention it to the office".** A verbal message gets lost. A lead in the system gets followed up.

The rules:

- **Only tag and raise what you found and photographed.** Never inflate a finding.
- **Tell the customer** what you've raised, so the follow-up call isn't a surprise.
- **Be specific.** "Hot water unit 10 years old, rust at the base, photos 4 and 5" is a lead. "Might want a new HWS" isn't.

For installers, extra work you raise that's sold and completed earns you 1%. [CONFIRM: exact install pay plan]

> **On the job:** Fitting new tapware in a Quakers Hill bathroom, you notice the hot water unit's label says it's ten years old, and there's rust at the base. You photograph both and tell the customer, who'd like a price. You raise it as a lead with your photos and notes. It isn't today's job, but it could be next week's.

## Leaving ServiceM8 habits behind

If you've worked in ServiceM8, here or somewhere else, some old habits will follow you. In ServiceTitan, they leave gaps.

The old habits, and what replaces them:

- **Texting photos to the office.** Save them to the job instead.
- **Notes on paper or in your own phone.** Write them in the job, on site.
- **Doing your times at the end of the week.** Statuses and timesheets as you go.
- **Typing your own price.** Pricebook lines and approved templates only.
- **One price for the job.** Four options, Elite first.
- **"I'll tell the office about it."** A tag or a lead, with photos and notes.
- **Going in cold.** History checked before you knock.
- **Sorting the paperwork at home.** Invoice, payment and sign-off before you leave.

Everyone in the business works from the same screen. Dispatch, the office, Harriet following up quotes, Chloe making happy calls and the Install Manager all rely on what you put in. One tech working around the system leaves gaps that everyone else falls into.

Old habits come back when you're busy. Pick the one you slip into most, and fix that first.

> **On the job:** After a bathroom job in Strathfield, you text five photos to the office out of habit. They never make it onto the job. Months later, the customer queries the work and nobody can find them. Saved to the job, they'd have been there for anyone who needed them.
