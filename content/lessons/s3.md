## Machine choice: hand tools, electric eel, jetter

Choose the machine to suit the blockage, the pipe and the access, not whichever one is easiest to reach in the van.

- **Hand tools** (plunger, hand auger): for a blockage in one fixture or its trap, like a sink, basin or toilet.
- **Electric eel**: a motor-driven cable with cutting heads. It's the go-to for roots and hard blockages in the main line.
- **Jetter**: high-pressure water that scours the pipe walls and flushes debris away. It suits grease, fat, sludge, silt and sand, and flushing out roots once they've been cut.

First work out where the blockage is. One fixture blocked points to that fixture's branch. Several fixtures blocked, or the overflow relief gully overflowing, points to the main line.

**If you suspect the pipe is damaged, run the camera first.** Forcing a cutter through a cracked or collapsed pipe can make it worse.

Sewage is a health risk, so wear the right PPE and follow the hygiene rules from Field Foundations. [CONFIRM: which drain machines and cameras each service truck carries]

## CCTV inspection, locating and recording footage

**Every recurring blockage gets a camera inspection, and the footage is what the customer decides on.** [CONFIRM: whether the camera inspection on a recurring blockage is part of the visit or a pricebook line the customer approves first]

1. Clear the line first where you can, so the camera shows the pipe, not the blockage.
2. Record from the moment the camera goes in until it comes out. If you talk over the footage, keep it calm and factual: a strata manager or an insurer may watch it later.
3. Note the distance on the counter at every problem: roots at a joint, a dip, a crack.
4. **Locate it.** Find the camera head from above ground with the locator, then mark the spot and the approximate depth. Photograph the mark with something fixed in the shot, like the back step.
5. **Save the footage to the job in ServiceTitan**, never just on your own phone. Number your photos and refer to them in your notes.

Locating matters because the fix depends on where the problem is: under the lawn, the driveway or the slab. It also shows how close it is to the boundary.

> **Say it like this:** "Let me show you what's going on in there. This is your sewer pipe, about eight metres from the house, and those are roots coming in through the joint."

## Roots, bellies, cracked and collapsed pipes

Most recurring blockages come down to one of four things. Learn to spot them on the screen and explain them simply.

- **Roots** get in through joints and cracks, chasing water. Clearing cuts them back, but they grow back.
- **Bellies** are sections where the pipe has sagged. Waste settles in the dip until it blocks again. Clearing removes the build-up, not the dip.
- **Cracked pipes** let roots in and wastewater out, and they tend to get worse.
- **Collapsed pipes** have broken in. The camera may not get past, and the line may not clear at all.

**A clear is temporary if roots or damage are present. Say so**, then present all four options. It's the honest answer, and it's usually why they keep calling.

Some older properties have asbestos cement (AC) pipe. If you suspect it, stop, photograph it, flag it in ServiceTitan and call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) or the office. Never cut, drill or break it.

> **On the job:** At an older house in Beecroft, the footage shows a root mass at a joint about six metres out, and the pipe dips just past it. That's two causes, not one, and the customer needs to see both before they choose.

## Options: re-clear, spot repair, reline, dig and replace

Once the customer has seen the footage, there are four ways to deal with a damaged drain:

- **Re-clear**: fixes today, but with roots or damage there, it will block again.
- **Spot repair**: fixes one damaged section, like a cracked joint.
- **Reline**: a new pipe formed inside the old one, with little or no digging. [CONFIRM: whether we do relines in-house or book them out]
- **Dig and replace**: new pipe for the damaged section. Where a pipe has collapsed, it can be the only real fix.

Build the four options from what the footage shows, starting from the Drain Fix-for-Good bundle. Essential clears the blockage. Standard adds the CCTV inspection, saved to the job. Premium adds root treatment, a gully check and a spot repair where needed. Elite adds a reline or repair of the damaged section, with scheduled jetting under the Care Plan.

**Present Elite first, without pressure.** Doing nothing for now is also a choice, and it's theirs.

Every line comes from the pricebook. No repeat-customer discounts and no bundle discounts.

> **Say it like this:** "Clearing it again will get you going today, but with those roots in the joint it'll block again. Here are your options, starting with the one that fixes it for good. Leaving it for now is a choice too, and that's up to you."

## Overflow relief gullies and boundary responsibility [VERIFY Sydney Water rules]

The overflow relief gully is the grated outlet outside, usually near the bathroom or laundry. If the sewer blocks, it lets sewage overflow outside instead of up through the toilet or floor waste inside. It only works if:

- the grate sits loosely in place, not glued, screwed down or covered
- it hasn't been buried by a garden bed, paving or a deck
- it sits lower than the fixtures inside, but high enough that rain and runoff can't flow in [VERIFY: current AS/NZS 3500 and Sydney Water rules on gully height and position]

Check it on every drain job and photograph what you find.

**Who's responsible for which pipe decides who pays.** In general, the owner looks after the private sewer pipes on their property and Sydney Water looks after its main. Exactly where one ends and the other begins is set by Sydney Water. [VERIFY: Sydney Water's current rules on responsibility up to and at the point of connection to the main]

So locate the problem, measure it and photograph the mark. Never say "that's Sydney Water's problem" until you're sure. If several neighbours are affected, call the office before you go further.

> **On the job:** In Kogarah, the gully grate is under a new pebble garden bed, and the last blockage came up through the bathroom floor waste. Photograph it, explain it and include fixing it in your options.

## Storm season: stormwater vs sewer

**Blocked drains spike in storm season, from summer into autumn.** Go into it ready: van stocked, machines working, camera charged.

Stormwater and sewer are separate systems. **Stormwater** carries rain from roofs, gutters, downpipes and paving to the street or the council's system. **Sewer** carries wastewater from toilets, showers, sinks and the laundry to Sydney Water's main. They must stay separate. [VERIFY: current Sydney Water rules on stormwater getting into the sewer]

In a storm, ask what's overflowing, and when:

- A downpipe, gutter or stormwater pit overflowing in heavy rain usually means a stormwater blockage: leaves, silt or roots.
- A gully overflowing only when it rains can mean rainwater is getting into the sewer, through a cracked pipe, a low gully or a downpipe draining into it.
- Sewage overflowing in any weather is a sewer blockage.

If you find a downpipe running into the sewer, record it as an observation and explain it calmly. Never call it "illegal".

Storm season is also when water gets into homes, so photograph everything. Your notes may end up with an insurer.

> **On the job:** It's a wet February night in Engadine and the customer says "the drains are blocked". The gully is overflowing, but only since the rain started, and a downpipe empties right beside it. Before you reach for the eel, work out which system you're dealing with.
