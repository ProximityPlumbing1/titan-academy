## Site risk assessment and SWMS before you start

Before a single tool comes out, stop and look at the site. **Every job starts with a site risk check: what could hurt you, the customer or anyone else, and what you'll do about it.**

Walk the work area and ask:

- How do I get in and out safely? Stairs, ladders, wet paths, tight side passages.
- What's live? Water, gas and power, and where each one isolates.
- What's the building made of? Older homes can hide asbestos.
- Is there a pit, subfloor or roof involved?
- Who else is around, and what's the weather doing?

Then decide how you'll control each risk, and record it. Higher-risk work also needs a **Safe Work Method Statement (SWMS)**: a short written plan of the steps, the hazards and the controls. ## The right PPE on every job

**The right PPE, for the task in front of you, on every job.** Not just the big ones, and not just when the customer is watching.

PPE is the last line of defence. It doesn't remove the hazard, it stops it reaching you. Match it to the task:

- **Safety boots** on every site.
- **Eye protection** for cutting, grinding, drilling and jetting.
- **Gloves** for sharp edges, heat, chemicals and sewage.
- **Hearing protection** for loud tools, especially in small rooms.
- **A fitted dust mask or respirator** when you cut or drill brick, concrete, tile or plaster.
- **Sun protection** outdoors: a hat, long sleeves, sunscreen and water.
- **Hi-vis** on or near a road, or wherever a site requires it.

## Isolate water or gas before starting

**Isolate and test water and gas before you start. No exceptions.**

Isolating means shutting the supply off at the right point. Testing means proving it's actually off. A valve that looks closed but isn't is how ceilings come down and people get hurt.

1. **Tell the customer** what's going off, and roughly for how long.
2. **Shut it off** at the fixture's isolation valve, the stop tap, or the gas meter or appliance valve.
3. **Test it.** For water, open a tap downstream and check the flow stops completely. For gas, follow the test your gas licence requires. [VERIFY: current NSW gas isolation and testing requirements]
4. **Stop it being turned back on.** If a tenant or building manager could open it, tag it or tell them.
5. **Test again when you reconnect**, and leak-test gas before you leave.

Electric hot water units need the power isolated too, by someone licensed to do it. [VERIFY: who can disconnect and reconnect electric water heaters in NSW]

Valve won't fully shut off? Don't work it "wet". Go to the next isolation point, and if that affects other people, call the office first.

Your sign-off includes showing your lead tech how you isolate and test water and gas.

> **On the job:** A tap swap in a Chatswood unit. You close the valve under the sink, open the tap, and it keeps running. The next shut-off is the building's main, which affects every unit. You stop, photograph the valve and call the office before you touch the main.

## Asbestos awareness in Sydney homes (fibro, AC pipe, old vinyl): stop and ask if unsure

Plenty of older Sydney homes contain asbestos. Left alone and in good condition, it's low risk. **Cut, drill or break it and the fibres get into the air, and into your lungs.** The diseases it causes can take decades to show up, and they can kill.

Where plumbers find it:

- **Fibro**: asbestos cement sheeting on eaves, walls, bathrooms and laundries.
- **AC pipe**: old asbestos cement water, sewer and stormwater pipes.
- **Old vinyl**: old floor tiles and sheet vinyl backing, often around toilets and in laundries.

**You can't tell by looking.** Only a test confirms it, so treat anything that could be asbestos as asbestos.

If you suspect it:

1. **Stop.** Never cut, drill or break it. Not "carefully", not wetted down, not "just one hole".
2. **Photograph it.**
3. **Flag it in ServiceTitan.**
4. **Call the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) or the office.**

Testing and removal are specialist work with their own licensing rules. [VERIFY: NSW rules on who can test and remove asbestos] Don't ask the customer whether it's asbestos: they can't tell either.

> **Say it like this:** "Some materials in older homes need testing before anyone cuts into them. I'm going to pause this part and check with the office on the next step."

> **On the job:** Replacing a toilet in Bexley, you find the pan sits on old vinyl tiles and the wall behind is fibro. The new pan connector needs a hole drilled. You stop, photograph both, flag it and call.

## Confined spaces, pits and roof work

Pits, tight spaces and roofs carry some of the most serious risks in plumbing. **If you're not trained, equipped and sure it's safe, you don't go in or up.**

**Confined spaces.** Manholes, deep pits and tanks can hold gases that overcome you without warning, or too little oxygen to breathe. Entry has its own training and permit rules. [VERIFY: NSW confined space definition, training and permit requirements] If you're not trained and authorised, you don't go in. And never go in after someone who has collapsed: call 000.

**Subfloors.** Even when they aren't confined spaces in law, they can hide live cables, sharp debris, old fibro offcuts and spiders. Tell someone where you are, and know your way out.

**Pits and trenches.** Barricade or cover an open pit, and never leave it unattended, especially with kids or pets around.

**Roofs and ladders.** Use the right ladder, secured on firm, level ground. Never step on fibro roofing or skylights: they can give way. If the roof is wet, steep or out of reach, or the job needs edge protection or a harness, stop and call the office. [VERIFY: NSW rules for excavation depth and fall protection on roofs]

> **On the job:** A leaking flashing on a two-storey home in Turramurra. Your ladder barely reaches the gutter and the roof is still wet from the morning rain. The customer says the last plumber "just went up". You don't. You photograph the setup and call the office to arrange the right access.

## Hot works and gas safety

**Hot works** means any flame, heat or sparks: soldering, brazing, a torch or a grinder. They can start a fire you won't see until it's too late, inside a wall cavity, a roof space or a subfloor.

Before you light up:

- Move or shield anything that can burn, behind and around the joint.
- Use a heat shield or fire blanket, and keep an extinguisher within reach.
- Outside? Check it isn't a total fire ban day. [VERIFY: NSW total fire ban rules for hot works]

When you finish, stay and check. A cavity can smoulder long after the flame is out.

**Gas.** Only work on gas if your licence covers it, and always isolate and test (lesson 3). [CONFIRM: which techs hold gas licences]

If you smell gas:

1. No flames, no smoking, and don't switch anything on or off.
2. If it's safe, turn it off at the meter.
3. Open up the house and get everyone out.
4. Call 000 if anyone is in danger, then the gas emergency line [VERIFY: the gas emergency number techs should use], then the office.

> **Say it like this:** "I can smell gas, so I'm turning it off at the meter. Can you take everyone outside for me while I sort it out?"

> **On the job:** Brazing a joint on a hot water replacement in a Hunters Hill weatherboard, you see the cavity behind it is full of old insulation and dry timber. Shield first, extinguisher beside you, and check the cavity again before you pack up.

## Sewage, hygiene and PPE

Sewage carries bacteria and viruses that can make you seriously ill, like gastro and hepatitis. They get in through your mouth, eyes, nose and cuts. **Treat every sewage job as contaminated, and protect yourself and the customer's home.**

PPE for sewage work:

- **Waterproof gloves**, heavy-duty enough not to tear.
- **Eye protection or a face shield** when jetting or clearing a blockage. Lines can blow back.
- **Waterproof boots**, and overalls you can take off before you get back in the van.
- **Waterproof dressings** over any cuts before you start.

Hygiene habits:

- **Wash your hands** before you eat, drink, smoke, touch your face or get back in the cab.
- **Keep dirty gear separate** from clean gear, and out of the cab.
- **Clean your tools** and the jetter after the job.
- **Don't carry it through the house.** Shoe covers, drop sheets, and keep the mess contained.

Ask your GP about the vaccinations recommended for plumbers who work with sewage. If an overflow has reached carpets, walls or living areas, photograph it and tell the office: the clean-up may be bigger than a plumbing job.

> **On the job:** Jetting a blocked sewer in a Rockdale backyard, the line clears and water blows back out of the gully. Your face shield takes it, not your face. Before lunch, you wash up properly and bag the dirty overalls away from the cab.

## Driving, fleet and fatigue

You spend a big part of every day behind the wheel, and a crash can do more harm than anything on site. **The van gets the same care as the work.**

**On the road**

- **Phone down.** Make the "on my way" call before you pull out, or hands-free only where that's legal. [VERIFY: current NSW mobile phone rules for drivers]
- **Drive and park like the customer is watching.** Sometimes they are.
- **Never drive or work affected** by alcohol, drugs or medication that makes you drowsy.

**The van**

- **Secure the load**: ladders tied down, nothing loose in the cab, and gas bottles upright, secured and turned off. [VERIFY: rules for carrying gas cylinders in an enclosed van]
- **Report defects, damage and any accident straight away.** [CONFIRM: how techs report vehicle defects, damage and accidents]

**Fatigue**

Long days, early starts, overtime and after-hours call-outs add up. Tired driving is dangerous driving, and tired work is where injuries happen. Watch for heavy eyes, yawning, drifting in your lane and missed turns.

**If you're too tired to drive or work safely, say so.** It's the same rule as stopping an unsafe job, and nobody is criticised for it.

> **On the job:** After a full day and an after-hours emergency, you're heading home from Rouse Hill and catch yourself drifting at the lights. You pull into a servo and rest before you drive on. Getting home late beats not getting home.

## Licences: what you can do licensed vs supervised (apprentices and trainees)

In NSW, plumbing, drainage and gasfitting are licensed work. **Your licence or certificate says exactly what you can do, and whether you can do it on your own or only under supervision.** [VERIFY: current NSW licence and certificate categories for plumbing, drainage and gasfitting]

**Only do work your licence covers.** If a job needs something outside it, like gas work without a gas licence, or electrical work you're not licensed for, stop and raise it. Don't "just do it while you're there", even if the customer asks. Know what your licence covers and when it expires, so it never lapses.

**Apprentices and trainees** follow the same rule: they only do work their licence covers. And **supervised workers have a named supervisor on every job.** Nobody should be guessing who that is. How close the supervision has to be depends on the work. [VERIFY: NSW supervision requirements for apprentices and trainees]

If you're the supervisor, that work is your responsibility. Check it, and never leave an apprentice working beyond what's allowed.

In this program, apprentices earn a **Supervised Competency, Install** certificate, reissued as full certification once they're licensed.

> **On the job:** You and your apprentice are swapping a hot water unit in Blacktown. The customer asks you to look at a leak in the granny flat out the back while the apprentice "finishes off" the connections. Can they? Only if that work, at that level of supervision, is allowed. If you're not sure, the answer is no.

## Compliance paperwork and certificates lodged on time [VERIFY current NSW Fair Trading process]

Some plumbing, drainage and gas work has to be certified and lodged with NSW Fair Trading once it's done. Which work, which certificate and how quickly are set by the regulator, and the rules change. [VERIFY: which work needs a certificate, the lodgement deadlines and who gets a copy]

**Compliance paperwork is part of the job, not admin afterwards. Certificates are lodged on time.**

Why it matters:

- **It's the law.** Late or missing certificates put your licence and the business at risk.
- **It protects the customer.** Insurers, buyers and strata managers may ask for proof the work was done properly.
- **It protects you.** The certificate, your photos and your test results show the work was done right.

Do it as part of finishing the job:

1. **Complete the paperwork on site**, while the details and test results are in front of you.
2. **Record it against the job in ServiceTitan**, so anyone can see it's done.

## Incidents, near-misses and stopping a job

**Anyone can stop a job if it's unsafe. Nobody is ever criticised for it. If you're unsure, stop and ask.** That goes for an apprentice in their first week as much as the most senior tech.

An **incident** is when someone is hurt or something is damaged. A **near-miss** is when it nearly happened. **Report both.** A near-miss shows us the hazard before someone gets hurt.

If something happens:

1. **Make it safe.** First aid, isolate water, gas or power, and keep people clear.
2. **Get help.** Call 000 if anyone is seriously hurt or in danger.
3. **Tell the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim) or the office straight away**, and our WHS contact. [CONFIRM: who the WHS contact is]
4. **Photograph it and write down what happened** while it's fresh.
5. **Log it** in [CONFIRM: which SWMS/WHS system we use].

Some serious incidents must be reported to SafeWork NSW, with the scene left as it is. [VERIFY: which incidents must be notified to SafeWork NSW, and how]

Damaged a customer's property? Tell them honestly and straight away, and don't agree how it will be fixed or paid for until you've called.

> **Say it like this:** "I'm not comfortable carrying on safely, so I'm going to pause here and get some advice before I go any further."

> **On the job:** Stepping off a ladder onto wet tiles in a Willoughby laundry, you feel it skid. You're fine. That's a near-miss, so you report it today, and next time you dry the floor and get the ladder footed.
