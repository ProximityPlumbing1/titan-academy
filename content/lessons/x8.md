## Who buys commercial: strata managers and committees, property managers and agencies, builders, commercial owners [CONFIRM target segments]

Commercial and strata buyers aren't the homeowner at the kitchen table. There are four kinds [CONFIRM: which commercial segments we're targeting]:

- **Strata managers and committees**, deciding for a whole building.
- **Property managers and agencies**, approving work for a landlord, within a limit.
- **Builders**, buying on behalf of their own client.
- **Commercial owners**, running a business in the building.

What they have in common: **they want reliability, compliance and response time.** And the person you meet is rarely the only one who decides. So **the quote has to work for people who weren't in the room**: the committee, the landlord, the owner, the accounts team. If it only makes sense with you standing next to it, it isn't finished.

Two ideas help, in our own words. From Sandler: qualify pain, budget and decision before you present. What is the problem costing them, what's been set aside, and who decides, how and by when? From RAIN Group: show the impact and the "new reality", meaning what changes for the building once the work is done.

> **On the job:** A strata manager meets you at a 1980s block in Chatswood after the third burst on the hot water riser this year. She won't approve it herself, and none of the committee will ever see the plant room. What does your quote need to show so they can decide without you there?

You own the scope and the quote.** Ruby (commercial dispatch) dispatches commercial jobs. [CONFIRM: exactly how responsibility splits between the Sales Techs, Alex, Bella and Ruby]

What that means day to day:

- **The relationship is theirs.** Don't promise ongoing work, special arrangements or different terms. Pass opportunities on straight away.

The rules don't change because the customer is a company: four options with Elite first, every line from the pricebook, and no free discounts.

> **On the job:** A property manager from a Parramatta agency asks if you'd do "all our properties" at a better rate. You don't discuss price. You take her details, tell her the right person will call, and pass it to Alex or Bella before your next job.

## Strata: common property vs lot, formal multi-option quotes for committee approval, staged works programs (bundling at building scale)

**Common property or lot.** Always record which it is, because it changes who approves and who pays. Photograph what you found and where, but don't give a legal opinion on site about who's responsible. [VERIFY: how NSW strata rules split responsibility for pipes and hot water between common property and lots]

**Formal multi-option quotes for committee approval.** The committee chooses, so the quote has to stand on its own: findings and photos, all four options with Elite first, what each one fixes, what's in and out, and what happens if they do nothing. Every line from the pricebook, no bundle discount.

**Staged works programs.** This is bundling at building scale. A building-wide fix can be a lot to approve in one go, so lay it out in stages: the urgent work first, the rest planned over time. Staging changes the timing, never the price of a line.

Up against other quotes? Agree up front what the committee needs to decide and by when. Compare scope like-for-like: a cheaper quote almost always has a smaller scope, so show the gaps. Offer to attend the committee meeting, and keep Bella in the loop.

> **Say it like this:** "Happy to help. What does the committee need to decide, and by when?"

> **On the job:** A Randwick block has three quotes for a building-wide hot water and riser problem, and ours is the most expensive. The committee meets next week. You compare the quotes with the chair line by line, offer a staged program with the failing risers first, and offer to attend the meeting.

## Commercial terms and approvals vs the 50% deposit [CONFIRM]

On residential planned work the rule is clear: **50% upfront on approval, before scheduling and materials, and the balance on completion.** You collect the deposit at signing.

Commercial and strata clients may work differently, and the terms that apply instead haven't been confirmed yet. [CONFIRM: what payment terms apply to commercial and strata work instead of the 50% deposit]

Until they are:

- **Don't agree to terms on site.** No "we'll just invoice you" and no "we'll skip the deposit".
- **Take it to Alex or Bella.** Terms are part of the relationship, and the relationship is theirs.
- **Get the approval in writing**, in whatever form the client uses, and record who approved it. Never go over a property manager's approval limit without written approval.

Approvals take longer when the decision sits with a committee, a landlord or a head office. Build that into the follow-up date you agree.

> **Say it like this:** "On planned work we normally take 50% on approval and the balance on completion. If your organisation needs something different, I'll have the right person in our office talk it through with you."

> **On the job:** The facilities manager of a childcare centre in Castle Hill wants to go ahead, but says their accounts team only pays on invoice. You don't agree and you don't argue. You note it on the job, tell her who'll be in touch, and pass it to Alex before anything is booked.

**You own the follow-up on your own quotes.** Harriet also chases open quotes when dispatch allows, so don't rely on it. A quote that goes cold comes off your close rate.

The standard:

- **At the time you agreed** before you left, not whenever you get round to it.
