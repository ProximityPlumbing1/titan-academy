## Keeping van stock up and restocking on time

Your van is your supply store. If a part isn't on board, it's a supply run, and supply runs burn the hours your pay depends on.

- **Know your stock list**, and what should be on board at all times. [CONFIRM: install van stock list]
- **Check stock against tomorrow's jobs the night before**, not at 7am in the driveway.
- **Restock on time.** Top up as you use things, not when the shelf is already empty. [CONFIRM: how install vans are restocked, and how often]
- **Record every material you use** against the job, from the pricebook. Unbilled materials come straight out of the profit.
- **Every tool has a home.** Searching for tools is lost time, and so is unloading half the van to find the one fitting you need.

A well-stocked, well-organised van is how you get first-time fixes and beat the estimate.

For this module's sign-off, a lead installer audits your van: **stock, returns and tools.**

> **On the job:** It's 5pm, and tomorrow you've got two tap swaps and a toilet suite in Cherrybrook. You check the van: you're down to your last pair of flexi hoses and there's no pan connector on board. What do you do tonight so you're still rolling at 7am?

## Returning unused parts

**Unused parts go back. Parts left in vans are lost profit.**

The business has already paid for every part in your van. While a spare part rolls around in the back, it isn't billed to a customer and it isn't back on the shelf for the next job. It gets knocked about, goes missing, or ends up used on another job without being recorded. Every one of those comes straight out of the profit.

What to do:

- **At the end of each job**, check what was ordered against what you used.
- **Return anything unused** the way the parts return process says. [CONFIRM: parts return process]
- **Note it against the job**, so the job's materials are right.
- **Specials matter most.** A part ordered for one particular job is the least likely to be used anywhere else.

Parts returned and van stock accuracy are one of the numbers installers are measured on.

> **On the job:** A hot water job in Liverpool came with two extra valves "just in case". You didn't need them. If they sit in the van for six months, they're money the business has spent on nothing. Return them this week, and note it against the job.

## Tool care and reporting damage

Tools are how you earn. Look after them and they look after your hours.

- **Every tool has a home**, and it goes back there after every use. Searching for tools is lost time, and a tool left on site is usually a tool lost.
- **Clean and check your tools** at the end of the day, so they're ready for the morning.
- **Check power tool leads and batteries** for damage before you use them. [VERIFY: test-and-tag requirements for power tools and leads]
- **Report damaged tools straight away** to the Install Manager (Charlotte, interim). [CONFIRM: how tool damage is reported and replaced]

A damaged tool is a safety risk and a delay waiting to happen. Don't keep using it until it breaks, don't hide it, and don't quietly buy a replacement yourself. Reporting it straight away means it's fixed or replaced properly, and nobody else picks it up thinking it's fine.

> **On the job:** Your press tool starts misfiring on a job in Rouse Hill. Pushing on risks a bad joint behind a wall, which becomes a warranty return. Stop using it, report it straight away, and get it sorted before tomorrow's jobs.

## Supplier accounts and pickups (Reece and others)

Suppliers like Reece are an extension of your van, as long as you use them the right way.

- **Plan pickups so they don't hold up a job.** Collect the day before where you can, so you're rolling at 7am fully stocked, not driving to the supplier halfway through a job.
- **Order specials ahead**, so they're ready when you get there.
- **Buy on the company account, against the job.** [CONFIRM: which supplier accounts we hold, and what to give and keep at the counter]
- **Note what you collected and for which job** in ServiceTitan, so the materials are billed and the job's costs are right.
- **Check it at the counter.** Make sure it's the right part and it's all there before you leave, not when you're back on site.

A pickup that's planned costs minutes. A pickup that's a surprise costs an hour of the job, and that hour is paid for by the business, not the customer.

> **On the job:** Tomorrow's job in Campbelltown needs a tempering valve you don't carry. Ordering it now and collecting it this afternoon means you're rolling at 7am. Finding out at 9am means a round trip to the supplier on the business's hours.
