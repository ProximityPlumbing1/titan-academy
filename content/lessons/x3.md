## Never prejudge: a modest house is not a modest budget, and you can't see wealth or debt from the driveway

Five years in, the money we lose comes from habit and assumption. The most expensive habit of all is **prejudging**.

A modest house or a worried face triggers a mental discount. Without meaning to, you decide what they can afford before they've seen a single number. You're guessing with your own wallet.

But you can't see wealth, or the lack of it, from the driveway. The tidy older home may have no mortgage. The flashy one may be drowning in debt.

**Prejudging is the most expensive objection, because we raise it for the customer.** They never got the chance to say yes.

> **On the job:** An older couple in a small fibro house in Seven Hills. You're tempted to quote the cheapest fix and move on. You present all four options instead. They choose Premium: "We've been meaning to sort this properly for years."

*Source: Proximity's October 2026 "Selling the Big Job" session.*

## Present every option, every time, at every door. Their choice reveals the budget, not your guess

The rule that kills prejudging: **present Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite every time.** No editing, no assuming.

The customer's choice tells you their budget far more accurately than your guess ever will. Some will choose Essential, and that's fine. But a surprising number will choose the option you'd have left out.

Don't read the options like a menu. **Lead with Elite**, with full confidence, then narrow to the two that fit this home.

> **Say it like this:** "The option I'd put in my own home is Elite. Let me show you why, and then what sits below it."

> **On the job:** Ask yourself the question from the October session: have you ever been shocked by who said yes to a big number? Then present every option at your next door and find out.

## Frame the system: an ageing system failing in stages. Patching one part moves the leak to the next weak point

Customers don't think in line items. When you hand over a list of five separate jobs, it looks like five separate bills.

So **frame it as one problem**: an ageing system failing in stages. Patching one part just moves the leak to the next weak point.

That's honest, because it's usually true. It's also how the customer actually experiences their plumbing: as one house that keeps having problems, not as a collection of parts.

> **Say it like this:** "Everything I've shown you is part of the same system, and it's all about the same age. Fixing just the one bit is fine for today, but the next weakest part is usually the next call-out."

> **On the job:** A 1970s home in Baulkham Hills: failed hot water unit, no tempering valve, perished flexi hoses and high mains pressure. That's not four jobs. It's one ageing system, and the photos show it.

## The bundling rule: never quote one job. Build the tiers as bundles from what you found (photos, video, CCTV)

**Never quote one job.** Every quote has at least one bundled option, built from what you actually found on site: the photos, the video and the camera footage.

How the tiers usually build:

- **Essential**: the fix they called about.
- **Standard**: plus the closest related item.
- **Premium**: plus the root cause.
- **Elite**: the full bundle, future-proofed, with a Care Plan.

Bundles come from findings, **never from a script.** If it isn't needed, it doesn't go in. A customer who catches you padding a quote will never trust another one.

> **On the job:** The Baulkham Hills home again. Essential is the unit only. Standard adds the tempering valve. ## The five standard bundles: Hot Water, Whole-Home Water Protection, Bathroom Refresh, Drain Fix-for-Good, Landlord Compliance Pack

We have five standard bundles. Each has Essential, Standard, Premium and Elite contents, with Elite presented first:

- **Hot Water**: from a like-for-like unit up to an upgraded unit with valves and a Care Plan.
- **Whole-Home Water Protection**: from fixing the reported leak up to flexi hoses throughout, isolation valves, filtration and a Care Plan.
- **Bathroom Refresh**: from repairing one fixture up to new tapware, a toilet suite and a Care Plan.
- **Drain Fix-for-Good**: from clearing the blockage up to relining the damaged section, with scheduled jetting under a Care Plan.
- **Landlord Compliance Pack**: from fixing the tenant's issue up to water-efficient fittings throughout and an annual Care Plan. [VERIFY: current NSW water-efficiency requirements for tenant water billing]

They're starting points. Only offer a bundle when your findings support it.

## Build options around what they told you matters, not what we'd like to sell. Whole job or small first: high trust, go whole; low trust, earn it with one great job, never go small out of fear of the number

The options should reflect **what the customer told you matters**, not what we'd like to sell. If they said they're selling next year, Elite's future-proofing isn't the point. If they said they never want another emergency, it is.

Then read the room:

- **High trust: go for the whole thing.** They've started asking forward questions like "how long will it last?" and "when can you start?". Present the full bundle with confidence.
- **Low trust: earn it with one great job.** If they're guarded, a well-done first job is how you get the bigger one later.
- **Never go small out of fear of the number.** Going small because *you're* nervous about the price is just prejudging in disguise.

> **Say it like this:** "You mentioned you're planning to stay here a long time, so I've built these around doing it once and not thinking about it again."

## Pricing: every line from the pricebook, no bundle discount. Cheaper means fewer lines, never a lower price

**No bundle discounts, ever. Every line is priced from the pricebook.**

The value of a bundle isn't a lower price. It's one visit, one warranty and one handover, and the next failure is fixed before it happens.

If the customer wants it cheaper, they choose an option with **fewer lines**. They don't get the same work for a lower price. Otherwise they learn our first number was fake, and every quote after that starts with a haggle.

You can't discount, and only Harriet or Sadie can change a price. Never say "let me see what the office can do".

> **Say it like this:** "I can't do that price on the full job. If we do just the urgent part today, here's where we land."

## Turning repeat bundles into ServiceTitan templates

**If you've quoted the same bundle twice, it should be a template.**

Templates mean every customer gets the same price and the same wording for the same job, and building the quote is faster than starting from scratch at the kitchen table.

A good bundle template has standard scope wording, inclusions and exclusions, materials, and **realistic combined hours**. Remember that combined hours have to reflect how the installer will actually pace a bigger job. Under-scoping a bundle costs the installer and the margin.
