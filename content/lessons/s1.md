## What a Service Tech does at Proximity: diagnose, fix, sell on the spot

**Most customers meet Proximity through a service call.** For many of them, you're the first Proximity tradie they've ever met.

Your job has three parts, all on the same visit:

- **Diagnose.** Find the real problem and its root cause, not just the symptom.
- **Fix.** Fix what they called about, first time, and the same day where you can.
- **Sell on the spot.** Present all four options, Elite first, and let the customer choose. Work they choose for today gets done today. Larger work is booked as a planned job with the 50% deposit.

You attend reactive calls, emergencies, after-hours call-outs and preventive maintenance visits. Kim (residential dispatch) books your jobs. The Install Manager manages Service Techs too. Until that role is filled, Charlotte (Operations Manager) covers it, with a lead service tech signing off your technical skills.

**If you sell a planned job, you captain it**: the handover, the installer's questions and the finish. Charlotte, as interim Install Manager, owns payment and the customer's happiness.

> **On the job:** You clear a blocked toilet in Gladesville in twenty minutes. On the way out you notice a rusty 12-year-old hot water unit and perished flexi hoses under the vanity. The customer only asked about the toilet. What does doing your whole job look like here?

## Your KPIs: average job value, first-time fix, returns, reviews

You're measured on four main numbers:

- **Average job value**: what your jobs are worth on average. The goal is to lift it from $2,500 to $3,200. [CONFIRM: how average job value is defined and measured]
- **First-time fix**: how often you solve the problem on the first visit, with the right diagnosis and the right part on board.
- **Returns**: warranty and same-day returns.
- **Reviews**: genuine 5-star Google reviews.

**Average job value rises when you fix the whole problem and offer genuine options, not when you push.** It goes up because you found the perished hose and the ageing unit, and the customer chose to deal with them. It never goes up because you talked someone into something they didn't need.

**Every return trip is lost capacity.** While you're back at yesterday's job, the truck isn't at a new one.

You're also tracked on how often customers choose one of your options, and on Care Plans sold.

> **On the job:** Two techs each do four drain jobs in Parramatta. One clears them and leaves. The other clears them, shows the camera footage and presents four options, and two customers choose a permanent fix. Same hours, very different average job value.

## Dispatch Pro: how close rate × average ticket by job type decides which jobs you get [CONFIRM: live for service trucks?]

Dispatch Pro is the ServiceTitan tool that assigns jobs automatically. No favourites, no guesswork, just the data. Proximity introduced it at the October 2026 Sales Masterclass. [CONFIRM: whether Dispatch Pro is live for service trucks]

For every job, it picks the tech most likely to get the best result. It scores you on two numbers for that job type, multiplied together, over your last 30 days and your last 12 months:

- **Your close rate**: how often customers say yes.
- **Your average ticket**, compared with the median for that job type.

The rules it plays by:

- **Regular shifts only.** On-call shifts are ignored. Saturdays only count if they're rostered as regular shifts.
- **Skills on file.** You need every skill the job type lists. Check yours with dispatch (Kim).
- **Drive time is a hard limit.**
- **Recall and warranty jobs are left out.** Every other job feeds your score.

Because the numbers multiply, small lifts add up. Ten jobs at $2,000 is $20,000. Eleven at $2,200 is $24,200: **21% more**, from a 10% bigger ticket and one extra yes.

> **On the job:** Two techs both run hot water calls. One presents all four options every time. The other quotes a like-for-like unit and moves on. Over a few months, Dispatch Pro ranks the first tech higher for hot water, so they get matched to the jobs with the most potential.

## What great service techs do differently

Great service techs aren't pushier. They're more prepared, more thorough and better listeners:

- **They're ready the night before**, with tomorrow's jobs checked and the van stocked.
- **They tell the customer what's going to happen**: what they'll check, roughly how long, and that they'll explain the options before doing any work.
- **They listen more than they talk**, and diagnose the person as well as the plumbing: what's worrying them and what they want to avoid.
- **They check the whole home** and never inflate a finding.
- **They show, don't tell**, with photos and camera footage.
- **They present all four options, Elite first, at every door**, and never decide what a customer can afford.
- **They say the price, then stop talking.**
- **They finish properly**: paid, signed off and the Care Plan offered.

People buy peace of mind, not parts. That's what the best techs are really offering.

> **Say it like this:** "Here's what I'll do today. I'll sort out the tap first, then do a quick check of the rest of your plumbing. Before I do any extra work, I'll show you what I found and give you the options."
